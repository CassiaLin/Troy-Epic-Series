〔U0740 heading〕
第五章
> CHAPTER V.

〔U0741 heading〕
論潘妮洛碧是否遭到粉飾
> ON THE QUESTION WHETHER OR NO PENELOPE IS BEING WHITEWASHED.

〔U0742 para〕
古人之間流傳著種種有損潘妮洛碧名節的說法，這是眾所周知的；事實上，這些說法似乎在史詩集成完成之前就已盛行。一般認為，《忒勒戈尼亞》在年代順序上緊接在《奧德賽》之後，而在這部詩裡，奧德修斯殺了求婚者以後，並沒有繼續和潘妮洛碧一起過日子，反倒到忒斯普洛提亞定居，娶了該國的女王卡莉狄刻。如此說來，他必定已經休了潘妮洛碧；倘若他相信《奧德賽》對她品行的那套說法，這種事他恐怕很難做得出來。照《忒勒戈尼亞》作者的說法，潘妮洛碧和忒勒馬科斯繼續住在伊薩卡，後來奧德修斯終於回到那裡，卻被忒勒戈諾斯殺死──忒勒戈諾斯是喀耳刻為他生的兒子。至於古代其他有損她名節的說法（雖然年代晚了許多），讀者可進一步參閱《史密斯辭典》的「潘妮洛碧」條。
> It is known that scandalous versions of Penelope's conduct were current among the ancients; indeed they seem to have prevailed before the completion of the Epic cycle, for in the *Telegony*, which is believed to have come next in chronological order after the *Odyssey*, we find that when Ulysses had killed the suitors he did not go on living with Penelope, but settled in Thesprotia, and married Callidice, the queen of the country. He must, therefore, have divorced Penelope, and he could hardly have done this if he accepted the Odyssean version of her conduct. According to the author of the *Telegony*, Penelope and Telemachus go on living in Ithaca, where eventually Ulysses returns and is killed by Telegonus, a son who had been born to him by Circe. For further reference to ancient, though a good deal later, scandalous versions, see *Smith's Dictionary* under "Penelope."

〔U0743 para〕
且看《奧德賽》要我們相信──或者不如說，要我們囫圇吞下──的是什麼。據說有一百多個年輕人，同時瘋狂地愛上了一位據信已經守寡的婦人；在他們的追求告一段落之前，她恐怕很難還不到四十歲，而且已有一個成年的兒子。他們明知她對自己的殷勤極其厭惡，卻一連糾纏了她好幾年。他們愛她愛得如此發狂，在她作出選擇之前，壓根兒想不到要向別人求婚（卷二 205–207 行）。等她選定了，他們就走；在那之前，為了報復她對他們的狠心，他們要把她的兒子忒勒馬科斯吃得傾家蕩產。於是他們便著手這麼做；而潘妮洛碧既是模範妻子，又是模範母親，痛苦得肝腸寸斷，一半是為了丈夫的死，一半是因為她沒辦法把求婚者趕出家門。
> Let us see what the *Odyssey* asks us to believe, or rather, swallow. We are told that more than a hundred young men fall violently in love, at the same time, with a supposed widow, who before the close of their suit can hardly have been under forty, and who had a grown up son—pestering her for several years with addresses that they know are most distasteful to her. They are so madly in love with her that they cannot think of proposing to any one else (ii. 205-207) till she has made her choice. When she has done this they will go; till then, they will pay her out for her cruel treatment of them by eating her son Telemachus out of house and home. This, therefore, they proceed to do, and Penelope, who is a model both wife and mother, suffers agonies of grief, partly because of the death of her husband, and partly because she cannot get the suitors out of the house.

〔U0744 para〕
照理說，她只要在求婚者夜裡離去之後立刻把門閂上，第二天早上拒不開門，也就成了；因為求婚者從來不和潘妮洛碧睡在同一座宅子裡。他們分散在城裡各處過夜，而奧德修斯的宅邸顯然就坐落在城的中央；他們既然溫順到肯讓人趕出去，只要屋裡的人拿出一點強硬的姿態，他們也就會溫順到肯讓人擋在門外。他們之中從來沒有一個人單獨見過潘妮洛碧；她每次出現在他們面前，總有兩名端莊的女僕隨侍，一左一右站在她身旁，而她則端端莊莊地拿一塊面紗或屏障遮在臉前──不錯，她已經四十歲了，可是她本人也好，女詩人也好，似乎都沒把這件事放在心上，所以我們可以斷定，讓她舉起面紗的確實是端莊，不是別的什麼。求婚者並不是講究分寸的人，他們儘管對潘妮洛碧一往情深，卻和她的女僕們有不正當的親密關係──而這些女僕事情敗露之後，似乎沒有一個立刻被逐出家門。說來有點奇怪：那些遠道而來的求婚者，竟然沒有一個堅持要在宅子裡連宿帶食；作者又費盡心思，唯恐潘妮洛碧沾上一絲一毫的流言，以致我們不禁推斷，作者自覺有必要把這份用心擺得明明白白。舉例來說，倘若潘妮洛碧真有作者竭力描繪的一半貞潔，我想第二十三卷裡她對奧德修斯歸來一事，就不會被寫得那樣半信半疑。作者給她安上的謹慎有多少，在某種程度上正好可以拿來衡量：作者認為非塗上不可的那層白粉究竟有多厚。潘妮洛碧對丈夫的種種忠貞之中，始終讓人覺得「這位夫人表白得太過火了」。
> One would have thought all she had to do was to bolt the doors as soon as the suitors had left for the night, and refuse to open them in the morning; for the suitors never sleep in the same house with Penelope. They sleep at various places in the town, in the middle of which Ulysses' house evidently stands, and if they were meek enough to let themselves be turned out, they would be meek enough to let themselves be kept out, if those inside showed anything of a firm front. Not one of them ever sees Penelope alone; when she comes into their presence she is attended by two respectable female servants who stand on either side of her, and she holds a screen or veil modestly before her face—true, she was forty, but neither she nor the poetess seem to bear this in mind, so we may take it as certain that it was modesty and nothing else that made her hold up the veil. The suitors were not men of scrupulous delicacy, and in spite of their devotion to Penelope lived on terms of improper intimacy with her women servants—none of whom appear to have been dismissed instantly on detection. It is a little strange that not one of those suitors who came from a long distance should have insisted on being found in bed as well as board, and so much care is taken that not one breath of scandal should attach to Penelope, that we infer a sense on the writer's part that it was necessary to put this care well in evidence. I cannot think, for example, that Penelope would have been represented as nearly so incredulous about the return of Ulysses in Book xxiii., if she had been nearly as virtuous as the writer tries to make her out. The amount of caution with which she is credited is to some extent a gauge of the thickness of the coat of whitewash which the writer considers necessary. In all Penelope's devotion to her husband there is an ever present sense that the lady doth protest too much.

〔U0745 para〕
然而更奇怪的是，這群熱烈激情的愛人，竟然從不為了爭奪這位中年的完美典範而彼此爭吵。適者生存在他們的體系裡似乎毫無地位。他們沒有半點嫉妒的跡象，反倒像一個非常和樂的大家庭，肩並肩、臉貼臉地過日子：擲矛打靶、下棋、在院子裡剝山羊皮、燎豬毛、喝下數不清的酒，大體上就是天天大擺筵席。他們堅持潘妮洛碧總得嫁給某個人，至於那個幸運的某人是誰，倒是無關緊要。[註1]似乎沒有人認為她非嫁給自己不可。他們之中也從沒有一個人發覺自己毫無希望，就此告辭；於是事情一年又一年地拖下去──這段期間潘妮洛碧可沒有越活越年輕──求婚者為潘妮洛碧相思欲死，潘妮洛碧則只為了擺脫他們而急得要死。
> Still stranger, however, is the fact that these ardent passionate lovers never quarrel among themselves for the possession of their middle-aged paragon. The survival of the fittest does not seem to have had any place in their system. They show no signs of jealousy, but jog along cheek by jowl as a very happy family, aiming spears at a mark, playing draughts, flaying goats and singeing pigs in the yard, drinking an untold quantity of wine, and generally holding high feast. They insist that Penelope should marry somebody, but who the happy somebody is to be is a matter of no importance.[註1] No one seems to think it essential that she shall marry himself in particular. Not one of them ever finds out that his case is hopeless and takes his leave; and thus matters drift on year after year—during all which time Penelope is not getting any younger—the suitors dying of love for Penelope, and Penelope dying only to be rid of them.

〔U0746 para〕
就算求婚者愛上忒勒馬科斯出錢供應的美酒佳餚，並不亞於愛上他的母親好了；可是這種完美情人兼完美食客的混合體根本不可能存在，若不是自知陷入了極大的困境，誰也不會求助於這種寫法──不管他（或她）是誰。人若是在戀愛，就不會白吃白喝；若是白吃白喝，就不是在戀愛；兩者可以擇一，卻不能兼得。因此，《奧德賽》的作者不但把這種不可能的行為加在求婚者身上，還要我們相信：一個聰明的女人，面對一百個愛人，即使他們多年來賴在她家裡，令她厭惡至極，她竟連其中寥寥幾個都擋不在門外──到了這個地步，我們就可以明白，作者正竭盡所能地矇騙我們；我們也就很願意相信，求婚者並不像我們被告知的那樣黑，潘妮洛碧也不像那樣白。
> Granted that the suitors are not less in love with the good cheer they enjoy at Telemachus's expense, than they are with his mother; but this mixture of perfect lover and perfect sponger is so impossible that no one could have recourse to it unless aware that he (or she) was in extreme difficulty. If men are in love they will not sponge; if they sponge they are not in love; we may have it either way but not both; when, therefore, the writer of the *Odyssey* not only attributes such impossible conduct to the suitors, but asks us also to believe that a clever woman could not keep at any rate some few of her hundred lovers out of the house, although their presence had been for many years in a high degree distasteful to her, we may know that we are being hoodwinked as far as the writer can hoodwink us, and shall be very inclinable to believe that the suitors were not so black, nor Penelope so white, as we are being given to understand.

〔U0747 para〕
至於說她被求婚者嚇住了，她有時候對他們說話可相當不客氣，例如卷十八 274–280 行；又如卷十九 322 行，她說起話來，彷彿完全有能力把任何一個惹她討厭的求婚者打發走。
> As for her being overawed by the suitors, she talks very plainly to them at times, as for example in xviii. 274-280, and again in xix. 322 where she speaks as though she were perfectly able to get rid of any suitor who was obnoxious to her.

〔U0748 para〕
不僅如此，我們還可以推斷：一個能板著臉講出這種故事的作者，對於男人如何對待自己所愛的女人，絕不可能有絲毫概念；對於女人通常如何看待自己所選中的男人（就我斗膽所能判斷的而言），也所知不多。因此我斷定，她當時還很年輕，而且尚未出嫁。無論如何，上面講的這個故事不可能出自荷馬之手；如果真是男人寫的，那必定是某位史前的安傑利科修士，他要嘛年輕時所知之少，要嘛老年時所忘之多，都遠非《伊里亞德》的作者所可能及。倘若他所知的仍足以寫出《奧德賽》，那麼他記得的東西，就會比《奧德賽》作者所顯露出來曾經知道的還要多。
> Over and above this we may infer that the writer who can tell us such a story with a grave face cannot have even the faintest conception of the way in which a man feels towards a woman he is in love with, nor yet much (so far as I may venture to form an opinion) of what women commonly feel towards the man of their choice; I conclude, therefore, that she was still very young, and unmarried. At any rate the story told above cannot have been written by Homer; if it is by a man at all it must be by some prehistoric Fra Angelico, who had known less in his youth, or forgotten more in his old age, than the writer of the *Iliad* is at all likely to have done. If he had still known enough to be able to write the *Odyssey*, he would have remembered more than the writer of the *Odyssey* shows any signs of having ever known.

〔U0749 para〕
一個男人，如果突發奇想（已故的丁尼生勳爵就很可能這麼做），要不顧當時流傳的種種醜聞，把潘妮洛碧寫成貞潔的女人──一個男人，是根本不會把求婚者寫成一群愛人的。他會立刻看出這絕不可行，而會把他們寫成一夥純粹的強盜，用威脅來壓制潘妮洛碧，只靠她天生的機智，再加上（譬如說）求婚者當中三四個暗地裡的盟友，才把他們牽制住。不管他怎麼寫，都無法讓求婚者日日長駐宅中顯得合情合理，但至少還有可能；反之，《奧德賽》作者端給我們的這種完美食客加完美情人的組合，卻荒唐得絕無可能。而且我想，若不是她一心要抬高自己的性別，要顯示一個聰明的女人如何能讓任意多的男人拜倒在她腳下，矇騙他們、寵壞他們，最後毀掉他們，她也不會要我們接受這種寫法。可是這肯定是女人的題材，而不是男人的──至少我不知道有哪一位男作家嘗試過類似的東西。
> A man, if he had taken it into his head (as the late Lord Tennyson might very conceivably have done) to represent Penelope as virtuous in spite of current scandalous stories to the contrary—a man, would not have made the suitors a band of lovers at all. He would have seen at once that this was out of the question, and would have made them mere marauders, who overawed Penelope by their threats, and were only held in check by her mother wit and by, say, some three or four covert allies among the suitors themselves. Do what he might he could not make the permanent daily presence of the suitors plausible, but it would be possible; whereas the combination of perfect sponger and perfect lover which is offered us by the writer of the *Odyssey* is grotesquely impossible, nor do I imagine that she would have asked us to accept it, but for her desire to exalt her sex by showing how a clever woman can bring any number of men to her feet, hoodwink them, spoil them, and in the end destroy them. This, however, is surely a woman's theme rather than a man's—at least I know of no male writer who has attempted anything like it.

〔U0750 para〕
我們已經看過從潘妮洛碧的觀點所講的故事；現在且聽聽求婚者那一方怎麼說。這見於第二卷開頭，我要把安提諾俄斯的話引得比我的縮譯更完整一些。他先說潘妮洛碧多年來一直給每一個求婚者捎去討好的口信，對他們個個加以鼓勵（順帶一提，雅典娜在卷十三 379–381 行完全證實了他的話），接著又說：──
> We have now seen the story as told from Penelope's point of view; let us proceed to hear it from that of the suitors. We find this at the beginning of Book ii., and I will give Antinous's speech at fuller length than I have done in my abridgement. After saying that Penelope had for years been encouraging every single suitor by sending him flattering messages (in which, by the way, Minerva fully corroborated him in Book xiii. 379-381) he continues:—

〔U0751 para〕
「然後她還對我們耍了另一個花招。她在房裡架起一座大繡架，開始做一件極大的精細針線活。『心肝們，』她說，『奧德修斯確實已經死了；不過，請別逼我馬上改嫁；等一等──我可不願我的針線手藝湮沒無聞──等我替英雄拉厄爾特斯做好一塊壽衣，好在死亡帶走他的時候備用。他非常富有，要是下葬時連一塊壽衣都沒有，地方上的婦人們會說閒話的。』
> "And then there was that other trick she played us. She set up a great tambour frame in her room, and began to work on an enormous piece of fine needlework. 'Sweethearts,' said she, 'Ulysses is indeed dead, still, do not press me to marry again immediately; wait—for I would not have my skill in needlework perish unrecorded—till I have completed a pall for the hero Laertes, to be ready against the time when death shall take him. He is very rich, and the women of the place will talk if he is laid out without a pall.'

〔U0752 para〕
「她就是這麼說的，我們也答應了；於是我們能看見她整天在那匹大布上忙著，到了夜裡卻點著火把把針腳拆掉。她就這樣愚弄了我們三年，我們始終沒有識破；可是時光流逝，到了第四年，她有個知情的女僕告訴了我們，我們當場撞見她在拆自己的活兒；於是不管願不願意，她都只好把它做完。
> "This was what she said, and we assented; whereon we could see her, working on her great web all day, but at night she would unpick the stiches again by torchlight. She fooled us in this way for three years and we never found her out, but as time wore on and she was now in her fourth year, one of her maids, who knew what she was doing, told us, and we caught her in the act of undoing her work; so she had to finish it, whether she would or no.

〔U0753 para〕
「因此，求婚者這樣答覆你，好讓你和亞該亞人都明白：『把你母親送走，叫她嫁給她自己和她父親所選中的人。』因為她若再仗著雅典娜教給她的那些才藝，再仗著自己聰明，擺著架子折磨我們下去，我真不知道會出什麼事。我們從沒聽說過這樣的女人。堤羅、阿爾克墨涅、密克涅和古代那些有名的女人，我們都知道，可是她們沒有一個比得上你母親。她那樣對待我們是不公平的；只要她一天還抱著上天如今賦予她的這副心思，我們就一天接著吃你的家產；我也看不出她有什麼理由要改變，因為榮耀和光彩是她得了，損失這一大筆家財的卻是你，不是她。可是我們不會回去做自己的事，也不到別處去，直到她作出選擇，嫁給我們當中的某一個為止。」（卷二 93–128 行）
> "The suitors, therefore, make you this answer, that both you and the Achæans may understand: 'Send your mother away, and bid her marry the man of her own and her father's choice,' for I do not know what will happen if she goes on plaguing us much longer with the airs she gives herself on the score of the accomplishments Minerva has taught her, and because she is so clever. We never yet heard of such a woman. We know all about Tyro, Alcmena, Mycene, and the famous women of old, but they were nothing to your mother any one of them. It was not fair of her to treat us in that way, and as long as she continues in the mind with which heaven has now endowed her, so long shall we go on eating up your estate; and I do not see why she should change, for it is she who gets the honour and glory, and it is you, not she, who lose all this substance. We however, will not go about our business, nor anywhere else, till she has made her choice and married some one or other of us" (ii. 93-128).

〔U0754 para〕
那麼，大致說來，女作者的版本是：潘妮洛碧是受了冤屈的無辜者；求婚者的版本則是：她是個狡猾無情的調情高手，寧可有一百個仰慕者，也不要一個丈夫。哪一個版本比較接近──不是接近真相，因為我們可以確定，求婚者能說的遠比作者肯讓他們說的多得多──而是接近她正在加工、正按自己更喜歡的方式重講的那個原始故事呢？讀者想必已經注意到，這一回求婚者似乎在入夜之後還待在宅子裡。
> Roughly, then, the authoress's version is that Penelope is an injured innocent, and the suitors', that she is an artful heartless flirt who prefers having a hundred admirers rather than one husband. Which comes nearest, not to the truth—for we may be sure the suitors could have said a great deal more than the writer chooses to say they said—but to the original story which she was sophisticating, and retelling in a way that was more to her liking? The reader will have noted that on this occasion the suitors seem to have been in the house after nightfall.

〔U0755 para〕
我們不能忘記，忒勒馬科斯第一次向雅典娜說起求婚者時，承認他母親並沒有斬釘截鐵地說她不會改嫁。「她既不拒絕那可恨的婚事，」他說，「也不把事情做個了結。」（卷一 249、250 行）顯然沒有；可是如果沒有，為什麼沒有？不立刻拒絕，就等於招人追求；她如果無意招人追求，以她矇騙男人的高明手段，似乎早就能找到辦法「把事情做個了結」了。
> We cannot forget that when Telemachus first told Miranda about the suitors, he admitted that his mother had not point blank said that she would not marry again. "She does not," he says, "refuse the hateful marriage, nor yet does she bring matters to an end" (i. 249, 250). Apparently not; but if not, why not? Not to refuse at once is to court courtship, and if she had not meant to court it she seems to have been adept enough in the art of hoodwinking men to have found some means of "bringing the matter to an end."

〔U0756 para〕
給仰慕者捎些討人喜歡的小口信，可不是擺脫他們的好辦法。她可曾試過給他們碰釘子？史上沒有任何這類記載。她可曾說過：「好了，安提諾俄斯，不管我嫁給誰，你儘管放心，那人絕不會是你。」再來還有讓人厭煩這一招──她試過沒有？她可曾念她祖父的書信給他們聽？她可曾唱她自己作的歌給他們聽，或是彈奏她自己譜的曲子？每當我想擺脫什麼人，總覺得這些辦法很靈驗。確實有跡象顯示她在這方面下過一些功夫，因為求婚者說，她那些高雅藝術的胡鬧和唯美派的大話，他們再也受不了了；不過，她比較可能是想吸引人，而不是想把人嚇跑。她可曾把他們彼此在背後說的話添油加醋地轉述，挑得他們互相反目？她可曾請安提諾俄斯或歐律馬科斯坐下來給她的織物當模特兒──叫他們擺一個又僵又累的姿勢，非保持不可，同時嘴裡跟他們說個沒完？她可曾差他們去跑腿，然後又罵他們，說她根本不要他們？或者叫他們替她辦事卻忘了付錢，或者一再把他們打發回店裡去換東西，說他們付的錢多得太離譜，早知道她就自己去辦了？她可曾堅持要他們參加家庭禮拜？一句話，像她這樣精明的主婦，倘若真心不想被人追求，有成千種辦法可以信手拈來，她可曾用過其中任何一種？只要拿一點常識輕輕一碰，整座建築就化為齏粉。
> Sending pretty little messages to her admirers was not exactly the way to get rid of them. Did she ever try snubbing? Nothing of the kind is placed on record. Did she ever say, "Well Antinous, whoever else I may marry, you may make your mind easy that it will not be you." Then there was boring—did she ever try that? Did she ever read them any of her grandfather's letters? Did she sing them her own songs, or play them music of her own composition? I have always found these courses successful when I wanted to get rid of people. There are indeed signs that something had been done in this direction, for the suitors say that they cannot stand her high art nonsense and æsthetic rhodomontade any longer, but it is more likely she had been trying to attract than to repel. Did she set them by the ears by repeating with embellishments what they had said to her about one another? Did she ask Antinous or Eurymachus to sit to her for her web—give them a good stiff pose, make them stick to it, and talk to them all the time? Did she find errands for them to run, and then scold them, and say she did not want them? or make them do commissions for her and forget to pay them, or keep on sending them back to the shop to change things, and they had given ever so much too much money and she wished she had gone and done it herself? Did she insist on their attending family worship? In a word, did she do a single one of the thousand things so astute a matron would have been at no loss to hit upon if she had been in earnest about not wishing to be courted? With one touch of common sense the whole fabric crumbles into dust.

〔U0757 para〕
忒勒馬科斯在反駁求婚者時，沒有否認他們所說的任何一件事實。他沒有否認母親一向給他們捎去鼓勵的口信，也沒有試圖解釋她在織布一事上的行為。既然這一點已經承認，而潘妮洛碧在打發愛人們走路這件事上顯然也沒有盡到應盡的努力，我們怎能不懷疑某處有顆螺絲鬆了，懷疑有一個性質截然不同的故事正被人擺弄，好遷就作者的需要？倘若我們斷定，照原始版本的說法，潘妮洛碧拆她的織物，與其說是為了拖延一樁可恨的婚事，不如說是為了延長一段十分愜意的追求，難道會錯得很離譜嗎？
> Telemachus in his rejoinder to the suitors does not deny a single one of their facts. He does not deny that his mother had been in the habit of sending them encouraging messages, nor does he attempt to explain her conduct about the web. This, then, being admitted, and it being also transparent that Penelope had used no due diligence in sending her lovers to the right about, can we avoid suspecting that there is a screw loose somewhere, and that a story of very different character is being manipulated to meet the exigencies of the writer? And shall we go very far wrong if we conclude that according to the original version, Penelope picked out her web, not so much in order to delay a hateful marriage, as to prolong a very agreeable courtship?

〔U0758 para〕
拉厄爾特斯之所以搬到鄉下去住，不再進城（卷一 189、190 行），無疑是因為他看出了這裡正在發生什麼事；而潘妮洛碧之所以偏偏選了這種形式的活計，大概也是為了確保他不會走近她身邊。她為什麼不能替別人做壽衣呢？拉厄爾特斯每次來訪，都知道歐律克蕾亞只會告訴他，女主人正在樓上為他縫壽衣，馬上就下來──這樣他還可能繼續上門嗎？請讀者務必自己稍微想一想。
> It was no doubt because Laertes saw what was going on that he went to live in the country and left off coming into the town (i. 189, 190), and Penelope probably chose the particular form her work assumed in order to ensure that he should not come near her. Why could she not set about making a pall for somebody else? Was Laertes likely to continue calling, when every time he did so he knew that Euryclea would only tell him her mistress was upstairs working at his pall, but she would be down directly? Do let the reader try and think it out a little for himself.

〔U0759 para〕
至於說拉厄爾特斯像安提克蕾亞在第十一卷所說的那樣窮困潦倒，這話連一丁點真實都沒有。作者必須把他寫得很窮，才能解釋他為什麼沒有出面保護潘妮洛碧；可是潘妮洛碧做那匹布的藉口，卻是說他家財萬貫。求婚者的情形也一樣。要解釋忒勒馬科斯為什麼不設法向他們討回損失的時候，他們就窮得讓人覺得去告他們只是白花錢；反過來，作者要讓潘妮洛碧從他們身上弄到禮物、賣弄她女人的機智時（卷十八 274–280 行），也就是在奧德修斯殺死他們之前不久，他們又有花不完的錢了。再晚一天，她就來不及了。作者心裡很清楚這一點，但她可不打算讓潘妮洛碧錯失她的禮物。她顯然把男人看作可以任意獵取的對象，而男作家就不大會這樣看。不消說，她收到的第一件禮物是一件新衣裳。
> As for Laertes being so badly off as Anticlea says he was in Book xi., there is not one grain of truth in that story. The writer had to make him out poor in order to explain his not having interfered to protect Penelope, but Penelope's excuse for making her web was that he was a man of large property. It is the same with the suitors. When it is desired to explain Telemachus's not having tried in some way to recover from them, they are so poor that it would be a waste of money to sue them; when, on the other hand, the writer wants Penelope to air her woman's wit by getting presents out of them (xviii. 274-280), just before Ulysses kills them, they have any amount of money. One day more, and she would have been too late. The writer knew that very well, but she was not going to let Penelope lose her presents. She evidently looks upon man as fair game, which male writers are much less apt to do. Of course the first present she receives is a new dress.

〔U0760 para〕
回頭再說拉厄爾特斯：他必定有錢，否則奧德修斯怎麼會那麼富有？奧德修斯的錢是從哪裡來的？他在去特洛伊之前不大可能賺到多少，而且似乎也沒有從那裡寄過任何東西回家。家裡一直沒有他的音訊；而在第十卷，[註2]他似乎是隨身帶著自己那份戰利品回來的──果真如此，那些東西就在特里納基亞島外海的船難中損失了。他娶潘妮洛碧時似乎得過某種嫁妝，因為忒勒馬科斯說，他若把母親送走，就得把嫁妝退還給外祖父伊卡里俄斯，並以此作為不送她走的理由之一（卷二 132、133 行）；然而，奧德修斯的巨大財富，大部分必定來自拉厄爾特斯，而我們可以確定，拉厄爾特斯留給自己的，比給兒子的要多。那麼，這些錢都到哪裡去了？──因為拉厄爾特斯似乎是個生活極為節儉的人。答案是：錢仍在拉厄爾特斯手裡；至於他如今從不進城，原因一部分無疑是那塊壽衣，一部分是他兒媳過的那種醜聞不斷的生活，但主要是因為作者若不把他支開，就無法解釋他為什麼袖手不管。
> Returning to Laertes, he must have had money, or how could Ulysses be so rich? Where did Ulysses' money come from? He could hardly have made much before he went to Troy, and he does not appear to have sent anything home thence. Nothing has been heard from him, and in Book x.,[註2] he appears to be bringing back his share of the plunder with him—in which case it was lost in the shipwreck off the coast of the Thrinacian island. He seems to have had a dowry of some kind with Penelope, for Telemachus says that if he sends his mother away he shall have to refund it to his grandfather Icarius, and urges this fact as one of the reasons for not sending her (ii. 132, 133); the greater part, however, of Ulysses' enormous wealth must have come to him from Laertes, who we may be sure kept more for himself than he gave to his son. What, then, had become of all this money—for Laertes seems to have been a man of very frugal habits? The answer is that it was still in Laertes' hands, and the reason for his never coming to town now was partly, no doubt, the pall; partly the scandalous life which his daughter was leading; but mainly the writer's inability to explain his non-interference unless she got him out of the way.

〔U0761 para〕
再者，奧德修斯的母親在冥府向他講述伊薩卡近況的那番話（卷十一 180 行以下），也透露出有什麼事需要隱瞞。她對求婚者隻字不提。她只說忒勒馬科斯得應酬許多客人，而這也只是理所當然，因為他是地方官，自己也到處受人邀請（卷十一 185–187 行）。再沒有比這更冷淡的委婉說法了，也再沒有什麼比這更能顯示說話的人心知肚明：實際發生的事遠比她肯說的多。倘若安提克蕾亞相信兒媳是清白的，她早就把整個情況原原本本告訴奧德修斯了。
> The account, again, which Ulysses' mother gives him in Hades (xi. 180, &c.) of what is going on in Ithaca shows a sense that there is something to conceal. She says not one word about the suitors. All she says is that Telemachus has to see a good deal of company, which is only reasonable seeing that he is a magistrate and is asked out everywhere himself (xi. 185-187). Nothing can be more coldly euphemistic, nor show a fuller sense that there was a good deal more going on than the speaker chose to say. If Anticlea had believed her daughter-in-law to be innocent, she would have laid the whole situation before Ulysses.

〔U0762 para〕
或許有人會主張，那時求婚者還沒有大舉來到伊薩卡，因為冥府之行發生在奧德修斯漂泊的早期，在他與卡呂普索同住七年之前，所以安提克蕾亞或許真的對求婚者一無所知；可是作者忘了這一點，把忒勒馬科斯寫成已經長大成人。事實上，在這個時候，忒勒馬科斯頂多只有十二三歲，他要赴的宴、要辦的招待，頂多不過是一場兒童聚會而已。作者在年代上出了個紕漏，因為整部詩裡，忒勒馬科斯都被寫成在奧德修斯離家的第二十年才剛剛成年。顯然，作者描寫與安提克蕾亞會面的那一段時，心裡想的是奧德修斯歸來前夕的情況，那時求婚者正鬧得最兇。這一點在稍後更是明顯：同在冥府的阿伽門農說，特洛伊戰爭爆發時，忒勒馬科斯還是個抱在懷裡的嬰兒，如今想必已經長大成人了（卷十一 448、449 行）。
> It may be maintained that the suitors were not yet come to Ithaca in force, for the visit to Hades occurs early in the wanderings of Ulysses, and before his seven years' sojourn with Calypso, so that Anticlea may really have known nothing about the suitors; but the writer has forgotten this, and has represented Telemachus as already arrived at man's estate. In truth, at this point Telemachus was at the utmost only twelve or thirteen years old, and a children's party was all the entertainment he need either receive or give. The writer has made a slip in her chronology, for throughout the poem Telemachus is represented as only just arriving at man's estate in the twentieth year of Ulysses' absence. It is evident that in describing the interview with Anticlea the writer has in her mind the state of things existing just before Ulysses' return, when the suitors were in full riot. This, indeed, appears still more plainly lower down, when Agamemnon, also in Hades, says that Telemachus was a baby in arms when the Trojan war broke out, and that he must now be grown up (xi. 448, 449).

〔U0763 para〕
所以，就作者而言，奧德修斯母親的沉默是有意為之。在她的構想裡，安提克蕾亞必定對求婚者的事一清二楚──因為照安提克蕾亞自己的說法，她一直活到忒勒馬科斯年紀大得足以當地方官的時候才去世。我相信其中的解釋是：寫第十一卷的時候，作者還沒有打算添上第一至四卷，以及從第十三卷 187 行到第二十四卷的部分，而是打算對當時流傳的那些關於潘妮洛碧的醜聞置之不理，並且盡可能少提到她。等我談到這部詩的成形與發展時，再更詳細地處理這個問題；不過不妨現在就說明：上面指出的這個難題，除了解釋為一位把新作拼接到舊作上的年輕作者在年代上的疏忽之外，恐怕只能懸而不解。除了作者本人，任何人都會看出這個毛病而加以避免；事實上，她很可能後來也看出來了，只是覺得不值得費事去改。果真如此，至少我個人不會因此看輕她。
> The silence therefore of Ulysses' mother is wilful so far as the writer is concerned. She must have conceived of Anticlea as knowing all about the suitors perfectly well—for she did not die till Telemachus was, by her own account, old enough to be a magistrate. The explanation I believe to be, that at the time Book xi. was written, the writer had as yet no intention of adding Books i.-iv., and from line 187 of Book xiii. to Book xxiv. but proposed to ignore the current scandalous stories about Penelope, and to say as little as possible about her. I will deal with this more fully when I come to the genesis and development of the poem, but may as well say at once that the difficulty above pointed out will have to remain unexplained except as a slip in chronology on the part of a young writer who was piecing new work on to old. Any one but the writer herself would have seen it and avoided it; indeed it is quite possible that she came to see it, and did not think it worth her while to be at the trouble of altering it. If this is so I, for one, shall think none the worse of her.
