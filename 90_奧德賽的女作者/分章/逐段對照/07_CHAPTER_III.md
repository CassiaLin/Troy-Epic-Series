〔U0657 heading〕
第三章
> CHAPTER III.

〔U0658 heading〕
《奧德賽》中女性的壓倒性分量
> THE PREPONDERANCE OF WOMAN IN THE ODYSSEY.

〔U0659 para〕
在第一章裡，我已經回應了迄今為止有人向我提出的、針對我對作者性別看法的唯一一批 *à priori*（先驗的）反對意見；現在我要轉向剛才擺在讀者面前的這個故事，看看它本身提供了哪些出自女性之手的證據。
> Having in my first chapter met the only *à priori* objections to my views concerning the sex of the writer which have yet been presented to me, I now turn to the evidence of female authorship which is furnished by the story which I have just laid before the reader.

〔U0660 para〕
請容我一問：判斷作者是否為女性，最萬無一失的試金石是什麼？當然是女性的分量壓過一切，而且作者對女人通常要打交道的事物，比對那些多半屬於男人範圍的事物懂得更多。人總是偏愛寫自己最熟悉的東西，而人最熟悉的，正是自己本身最是什麼、最常和什麼打交道。這一點延伸到思考方式與性格上，比延伸到行動上還要明顯。倘若男人認為人類最高貴的研究對象是男人，那麼女人也同樣篤定地相信，那是女人。
> What, let me ask, is the most unerring test of female authorship? Surely a preponderance of female interest, and a fuller knowledge of those things which a woman generally has to deal with, than of those that fall more commonly within the province of man. People always write by preference of what they know best, and they know best what they most are, and have most to do with. This extends to ways of thought and to character, even more than to action. If man thinks the noblest study for mankind to be man, woman not less certainly believes it to be woman.

〔U0661 para〕
因此，在任何一部作品裡，如果女性寫得精彩而富於同情，男性卻寫得機械、相形之下敷衍了事，我想，我們就可以穩妥地推斷作者是女人；反過來，對男人也一樣成立。男人與女人從來不曾完全了解彼此，唯一的例外，或許是求愛與蜜月期間；而正如男人了解男人比女人所能了解的更透徹，女人了解女人也是如此。誠然，兩性都以盡可能了解對方為樂，而在這方面最成功的人，無論男女，都是最好也最幸福的人；但是，不管我們怎麼努力，這道藩籬永遠不可能完全打破，兩性各自主要──當然不是完全──都住在自己那個獨立的世界裡。再說，仔細想來，這道藩籬也不該打破，因為兩性彼此吸引的魅力，有很大一部分正是靠它的存在。
> Hence if in any work the women are found to be well and sympathetically drawn, while the men are mechanical and by comparison perfunctorily treated, it is, I imagine, safe to infer that the writer is a woman; and the converse holds good with man. Man and woman never fully understand one another save, perhaps, during courtship and honeymoon, and as a man understands man more fully than a woman can do, so does a woman, woman. Granted, it is the delight of either sex to understand the other as fully as it can, and those who succeed most in this respect are the best and happiest whether men or women; but do what we may the barriers can never be broken down completely, and each sex will dwell mainly, though not, of course, exclusively, within its own separate world. When, moreover, we come to think of it, it is not desirable that they should be broken down, for it is on their existence that much of the attraction of either sex to the other depends.

〔U0662 para〕
男人似乎沒有辦法描寫女人而不取笑她們或把她們漫畫化；同樣地，女人描寫男人，大概也從來沒有比把他寫得可笑時更傳神的了。她若存心把他寫得可笑，必定成功；她若無心如此，成功得還更有把握。其實兩性都能把對方寫成妙趣橫生的漫畫；而且，可以肯定的是，從來沒有哪位作者比《奧德賽》的作者更徹底地表明：除了頌揚女性之外，她認為男人的小毛病與小弱點，是最適合施展她天才的題材。不過我懷疑，在整個文學史上，有沒有哪位作者（我想莎士比亞要除外）曾經成功地為異性畫出一幅全身、真人大小、嚴肅的肖像。
> Men seem unable to draw women at all without either laughing at them or caricaturing them; and so, perhaps, a woman never draws a man so felicitously as when she is making him ridiculous. If she means to make him so she is certain to succeed; if she does not mean it she will succeed more surely still. Either sex, in fact, can caricature the other delightfully, and certainly no writer has ever shown more completely than the writer of the *Odyssey* has done that, next to the glorification of woman, she considers man's little ways and weaknesses to be the fittest theme on which her genius can be displayed. But I doubt whether any writer in the whole range of literature (excepting, I suppose, Shakspeare) has succeeded in drawing a full length, life-sized, serious portrait of a member of the sex opposite to the writer's own.

〔U0663 para〕
大家都承認，《伊里亞德》的興趣重心偏在男人一邊，《奧德賽》則偏在女人一邊。《伊里亞德》裡的女人為數不多，也很少站上舞台。不錯，女神們扮演了重要角色，但從來沒有人把她們當真。
> It is admitted on all hands that the preponderance of interest in the *Iliad* is on the side of man, and in the *Odyssey* on that of woman. Women in the *Iliad* are few in number and rarely occupy the stage. True, the goddesses play important parts, but they are never taken seriously.

〔U0664 para〕
再者，雪萊談到《伊里亞德》「最後七卷不斷攀升的壯麗」時說：「《奧德賽》是甜美的，但沒有任何東西比得上這個。」[註1]《奧德賽》的作者有時兇猛如母虎，但這部詩整體的感覺，正如雪萊所說。《伊里亞德》即使在最溫柔的段落，處處也都能感受到力量；而《奧德賽》從頭到尾令我們著迷的，與其說是力量，不如說是甜美。那是女人的魅力，而不是男人的。
> Shelley, again, speaking of the "perpetually increasing magnificence of the last seven books" of the *Iliad*, says, "The *Odyssey* is sweet, but there is nothing like this."[註1] The writer of the *Odyssey* is fierce as a tigress at times, but the feeling of the poem is on the whole exactly what Shelley says it is. Strength is felt everywhere even in the tenderest passages of the *Iliad*, but it is sweetness rather than strength that fascinates us throughout the *Odyssey*. It is the charm of a woman not of a man.

〔U0665 para〕
同樣地，再引一位較近的權威：格萊斯頓先生在前面提過的那部論荷馬的著作（即《荷馬與荷馬時代研究》）裡說（第 28 頁）：──
> So, again, to quote a more recent authority, Mr. Gladstone in his work on *Homer* already referred to, says (p. 28):—

〔U0666 para〕
在《伊里亞德》裡，宏偉或力量很少讓位給家庭親情的展現。反過來，在《奧德賽》中，家庭生活提供了織物的底布，詩的絲線便織進其中。
> It is rarely in the *Iliad* that grandeur or force give way to allow the exhibition of domestic affection. Conversely, in the *Odyssey* the family life supplies the tissue into which is woven the thread of the poem.

〔U0667 para〕
任何熟悉這兩部詩的人，必定都知道格萊斯頓先生說得對；他其實還可以補上一句同樣正確的話：在《伊里亞德》中，凡有家庭生活與親情的展現，男人總居於主導，女人則受他們保護；而在整部《奧德賽》裡，卻是女人在指揮、在出主意、在保護男人。
> Any one who is familiar with the two poems must know that what Mr. Gladstone has said is true; and he might have added, not less truly, that when there is any exhibition of domestic life and affection in the *Iliad* the men are dominant, and the women are under their protection, whereas throughout the *Odyssey* it is the women who are directing, counselling, and protecting the men.

〔U0668 para〕
《奧德賽》裡有哪些女人？有雅典娜，她無所不在，總守在奧德修斯與忒勒馬科斯的手肘邊，讓他們走正路，對他們時而責罵、時而奉承。在《伊里亞德》裡，她是偉大的戰士，卻不是女人；在《奧德賽》裡，她是偉大的女人，卻不是戰士。當然還有潘妮洛碧──幾乎直到最後都強勢專斷，而她一旦不再如此，幾乎立刻就被打發到側台去了。老女僕歐律克蕾亞也完全制得住忒勒馬科斯；她對他說：「孩子，沒有人可以怪罪的時候，就別怪罪了。」（卷二十 135 行）誰能懷疑海倫是墨涅拉俄斯家中的主人？她對墨涅拉俄斯能說的全部讚美，不過是他「無論相貌還是頭腦，都一點也不差」（卷四 264 行）。卷四的埃伊多忒亞，在整段普洛透斯插曲中，對墨涅拉俄斯都是 *de haut en bas*（居高臨下）。她待他和他的手下不錯，但他們必須完全照她吩咐的去做；而她顯然很享受「使喚」他們──我實在想不出更貼切的字眼了。主導全局的是卡呂普索，不是奧德修斯；而且請注意，無論她還是喀耳刻，住處似乎都沒有一個男僕。我有一次住在一家旅店，問那位氣派的老闆娘能否見見老闆。她把頭一昂，答道：「先生，我們這家店沒有老闆；我看不出男人在旅館裡有什麼用處，除了擦靴子和擦窗戶。」這正是喀耳刻與卡呂普索的口吻，只不過她們兩位似乎連這一點點讓步都沒有為男人保留。
> Who are the women in the *Odyssey*? There is Minerva, omnipresent at the elbows of Ulysses and Telemachus to keep them straight and alternately scold and flatter them. In the *Iliad* she is a great warrior but she is no woman: in the *Odyssey* she is a great woman but no warrior; we have, of course, Penelope—masterful nearly to the last and tossed off to the wings almost from the moment that she has ceased to be so; Euryclea, the old servant, is quite a match for Telemachus, "do not find fault, child," she says to him, "when there is no one to find fault with" (xx. 135). Who can doubt that Helen is master in the house of Menelaus—of whom all she can say in praise is that he is "not deficient either in person or understanding" (iv. 264)? Idothea in Book iv. treats Menelaus *de haut en bas*, all through the Proteus episode. She is good to him and his men, but they must do exactly what she tells them, and she evidently enjoys "running" them,—for I can think of no apter word. Calypso is the master mind, not Ulysses; and, be it noted, that neither she nor Circe seem to have a manservant on their premises. I was at an inn once and asked the stately landlady if I could see the landlord. She bridled up and answered, "We have no landlord, sir, in this house; I cannot see what use a man is in a hotel except to clean boots and windows." There spoke Circe and Calypso, but neither of them seem to have made even this much exception in man's favour.

〔U0669 para〕
請讀者去問問他認識的、住在自己房子裡的單身女士，她們比較喜歡讓男人還是女人伺候；如果他沒發現她們通常根本不想讓任何男人在屋裡走動──園丁當然除外──我會大感意外。不過話說回來，園丁通常有老婆，也有自己的房子。
> Let the reader ask any single ladies of his acquaintance, who live in a house of their own, whether they prefer being waited upon by men or by women, and I shall be much surprised if he does not find that they generally avoid having a man about the house at all—gardeners of course excepted. But then the gardener generally has a wife, and a house of his own.

〔U0670 para〕
再看瑙西卡：她固然可愛，但跟她唱反調可不是明智之舉；她知道什麼對奧德修斯有好處，只要他聽她的話，一切都會順順當當，但她必須是主人，他則是僕從。我發現我漏掉了卷五的伊諾。她不過是埃伊多忒亞的翻版，正如喀耳刻是卡呂普索的翻版，幾乎沒什麼變化。再問一次：誰是主人──阿瑞忒王后，還是阿爾基諾俄斯王？瑙西卡很清楚怎麼回答這個問題。她指點奧德修斯時說：
> Take Nausicaa again, delightful as she is, it would not be wise to contradict her; she knows what is good for Ulysses, and all will go well with him so long as he obeys her, but she must be master and he man. I see I have passed over Ino in Book v. She is Idothea over again, just as Circe is Calypso, with very little variation. Who again is master—Queen Arete or King Alcinous? Nausicaa knows well enough how to answer this question. When giving her instructions to Ulysses she says:

〔U0671 quote〕
「別管我父親，直接走到我母親面前，抱住她的膝蓋；只要她對你有好感，你就有幾分希望回到家鄉，再見到你的親友。」（卷六 310–315 行）
> "Never mind my father, but go up to my mother and embrace her knees; if she is well disposed towards you there is some chance of your getting home to see your friends again" (vi. 310-315).

〔U0672 para〕
在整段費埃克斯插曲中，阿瑞忒（Arēte）都比阿爾基諾俄斯重要──順帶一提，我認為她的名字是作者那些相當透明的偽裝之一，用意在讓人聯想到 Arĕte，也就是「美德」。我自己並不相信有她這個人；我相信，倘若阿瑞忒真像作者說的那麼好，潘妮洛碧就會被寫得更可親一些。不過，暫且把她擱在一邊：在《奧德賽》作者眼中，妻子比丈夫重要得多，重要到奧德修斯向費埃克斯人致告別辭時，她讓他說的是：願他們繼續讓自己的妻兒滿意（卷十三 44、45 行）；而不是願他們的妻兒繼續讓他們滿意。稍後，他又祝阿瑞忒王后與她的兒女、她的百姓，最後才是與阿爾基諾俄斯王，一切幸福美滿。至於阿爾基諾俄斯王，他幸不幸福無關緊要，只要他讓阿瑞忒王后滿意就行；不過，作為這樣一位令人讚佩的女人的丈夫，他想不幸福也難。
> Throughout the Phæacian episode Arēte (whose name, by the way, I take to be one of the writer's tolerably transparent disguises, and to be intended to suggest Arĕte, or "Goodness") is a more important person than Alcinous. I do not believe in her myself; I believe Penelope would have been made more amiable if Arete had been as nice a person as the writer says she was; leaving her, however, on one side, so much more important are wives than husbands in the eyes of the author of the *Odyssey* that when Ulysses makes his farewell speech to the Phæacians, she makes him say that he hopes they may continue to give satisfaction to their wives and children (xiii. 44, 45), instead of hoping that their wives and children will continue to give satisfaction to them. A little lower down he wishes Queen Arete all happiness with her children, her people, and lastly with King Alcinous. As for King Alcinous, it does not matter whether he is happy or no, provided he gives satisfaction to Queen Arete; but he was bound to be happy as the husband of such an admirable woman.

〔U0673 para〕
同樣地，約克公爵成婚的時候，我一再聽到女人說，希望瑪麗公主跟他在一起會非常幸福，卻從沒聽過哪個女人說，希望公爵跟瑪麗公主在一起會非常幸福。男人則說，希望這一對非常幸福，並不特別提到哪一方。
> So when the Duke of York was being married I heard women over and over again say they hoped the Princess May would be very happy with him, but I never heard one say that she hoped the Duke would be very happy with the Princess May. Men said they hoped the pair would be very happy, without naming one more than the other.

〔U0674 para〕
我已經把《奧德賽》中所有較突出的女性角色都簡略談過了。每一個例子的寓意似乎都是：男人懂得很少，而且即使在他懂得的那一點點事情上，也靠不住，難保不出洋相，除非手邊有個女人告訴他該怎麼做。全詩沒有一個例子是男人挺身而出，去解救身陷困境的美麗女子；情形總是恰恰相反。
> I have touched briefly on all the more prominent female characters of the *Odyssey*. The moral in every case seems to be that man knows very little, and cannot be trusted not to make a fool of himself even about the little that he does know, unless he has a woman at hand to tell him what he ought to do. There is not a single case in which a man comes to the rescue of female beauty in distress; it is invariably the other way about.

〔U0675 para〕
奧德修斯漂泊期間，給過他任何幫助的男性只有兩位：一位是埃俄羅斯，他並沒有真正幫上忙，而且第二次拒絕再幫他；另一位是赫耳墨斯，他給了奧德修斯藥草魔羅（卷十 305 行），保護他不受喀耳刻的魔法所害。不過在後一例中，我毫不懷疑作者是受了《伊里亞德》卷二十四那段可愛文字的誘惑──赫耳墨斯在那裡迎上普里阿摩斯，要引他到亞該亞人的營地去；事實上，上面提到的那段《伊里亞德》文字中，有一行漂亮的詩（還不止一行），被《奧德賽》的作者整句搬過來，用以描寫這位神的青春與俊美。[註2]除了這兩個例外，整部詩裡都是安德洛墨達救珀爾修斯，而不是珀爾修斯救安德洛墨達──是女基督徒做大勇先生的嚮導與守護者，而不是大勇先生做女基督徒的。
> The only males who give Ulysses any help while he is on his wanderings are Æolus, who does him no real service and refuses to help him a second time, and Mercury, who gives him the herb Moly (x. 305) to protect him against the spells of Circe. In this last case, however, I do not doubt that the writer was tempted by the lovely passage of *Il*. XXIV., where Mercury meets Priam to conduct him to the Achæan camp; one pretty line, indeed (and rather more), of the Iliadic passage above referred to is taken bodily by the writer of the *Odyssey* to describe the youth and beauty of the god.[註2] With these exceptions, throughout the poem Andromeda rescues Perseus, not Perseus Andromeda—Christiana is guide and guardian to Mr. Greatheart, not Mr. Greatheart to Christiana.

〔U0676 para〕
潘妮洛碧的情形似乎是個例外。也許有人會說，是奧德修斯前來解救她，而整部詩就繞著這件事打轉。但事實並非如此。奧德修斯殺死求婚者，第一是因為他們揮霍了他的家產──這從頭到尾都是最主要的怨恨；第二是因為他們姦污了他家中的女僕；第三，也只是第三，才是因為他還活著，他們就向他的妻子求婚（卷二十二 36–38 行）。從來沒有哪個女人比她更能在自己願意時守住自己的立場；而我很快就會詳細證明：她沒有守住的時候，是因為她寧可不守。
> The case of Penelope may seem to be an exception. It may be urged that Ulysses came to her rescue, and that the whole poem turns on his doing so. But this is not true. Ulysses kills the suitors, firstly, because they had wasted his substance—this from the first to last is the main grievance; secondly, because they had violated the female servants of his house; and only, thirdly, because they had offered marriage to his wife while he was still alive (xxii. 36-38). Never yet was woman better able to hold her own when she chose, and I will show at full length shortly that when she did not hold it it was because she preferred not to do so.

〔U0677 para〕
到目前為止，我談的是作者對陽世女性的態度。現在讓我們看看，在亡者的國度裡，她的本能驅使她認為什麼最有趣。奧德修斯到了黑帝斯的居所，遇見的第一個亡魂是他的同伴厄爾佩諾爾；此人喝醉了，就在奧德修斯一行即將啟航時，從喀耳刻家的屋頂上摔了下來。詩中明白告訴我們，他是個無足輕重的人，既不以頭腦也不以勇氣見長，所以讓他死了也無妨；而且顯而易見，這場意外之所以被允許發生，只是為了讓奧德修斯在冥府招呼這個亡魂時，能開個小玩笑，說厄爾佩諾爾走陸路，比奧德修斯走水路還早到。所以厄爾佩諾爾不算數。
> I have dealt so far with the writer's attitude towards women when in the world of the living. Let us now see what her instinct prompts her to consider most interesting in the kingdom of the dead. When Ulysses has reached the abode of Hades, the first ghost he meets is that of his comrade Elpenor, who had got drunk and fallen off the roof of Circe's house just as Ulysses and his men were about to set sail. We are expressly told that he was a person of no importance, being remarkable neither for sense nor courage, so that it does not matter about killing him, and it is transparent that the accident is only allowed to happen in order to enable Ulysses to make his little joke when he greets the ghost in Hades to the effect that Elpenor has got there more quickly by land than Ulysses had done by water. Elpenor therefore, does not count.

〔U0678 para〕

> Talking of *Homer* Fielding says, "I had the curiosity to ask him whether he had really writ that poem [the *Iliad*] in detached pieces and flung it about all over Greece, according to the report that went of him. He smiled at my question, and asked me whether there appeared any connection in the poem; for if there did he thought I might answer for myself." This was first published in 1743, and is no doubt intended as a reply to Bentley. See Jebb's *Introduction to Homer*, ed. 1888, note 1 on p. 106.
