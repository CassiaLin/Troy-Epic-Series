〔U1288 heading〕
第十五章
> CHAPTER XV.

〔U1289 para〕
《奧德賽》與特洛伊史詩集成其他詩篇的關係，以及它在女作者手中的發展
> THE ODYSSEY IN ITS RELATION TO THE OTHER POEMS OF THE TROJAN CYCLE, AND ITS DEVELOPMENT IN THE HANDS OF THE AUTHORESS.

〔U1290 para〕
《奧德賽》的作者似乎知道史詩集成裡大部分已經失傳、與特洛伊相關的詩篇──這類詩共有八部；不過，我們對這些詩的全部認識，只來自普羅克洛斯殘篇裡的提要，以及後世作者零星引用的幾行詩句，所以即使她提到的事件，普羅克洛斯明說是某一部詩所講述的，我們也無法確鑿無疑地斷定她手邊就有那部詩。儘管如此，從《奧德賽》卷一與卷三的若干段落看來，她很可能知道《歸返記》，也就是亞該亞人自特洛伊歸返的故事；我們也可以推想，涅斯托爾那幾篇長篇大論（《奧德賽》卷三 102–200 行與 253–328 行）主要就取材於此，因為其中有些細節，和普羅克洛斯所作的《歸返記》提要若合符節。
> The writer of the *Odyssey* appears to have known most of those lost poems of the Epic cycle—eight in number—that relate to Troy, but as all we know about them is from the summaries given in the fragment of Proclus, and from a few lines here and there quoted in later authors, we can have no irrefragable certainty that she had the poems before her even when she alludes to incidents mentioned by Proclus as being dealt with in any given one of them. Nevertheless, passages in *Od*. i. and iii. make it probable that she knew the *Nosti* or the Return of the Achæans from Troy, and we may suppose that Nestor's long speeches (*Od*. iii. 102-200 and 253-328) are derived mainly from this source, for they contain particulars that correspond closely with the epitome of the *Nosti* given by Proclus.

〔U1291 para〕
這樣我們就能解釋，為什麼涅斯托爾的話裡對愛琴海的地形描述得如此正確，而詩中其他地方，除了光知道提洛島上有阿波羅的神殿（《奧德賽》卷六 162 行），以及偶爾提一下克里特之外，就再也看不出這種知識。我看到傑布教授說，《奧德賽》「對提洛島顯出熟悉的認識」；[註1]可是詩中沒有任何一處足以支持這個斷言。
> We can thus explain the correctness of the topography of the Ægæan sea that is manifested in Nestor's speeches, but no where else in the poem beyond a bare knowledge of the existence of Apollo's shrine in Delos (*Od*. vi. 162) and an occasional mention of Crete. I see Professor Jebb says that the *Odyssey* "shows a familiar knowledge of Delos;"[註1] but there is no warrant for this assertion from anything in the poem.

〔U1292 para〕
《奧德賽》的作者在卷四裡，似乎也知道《庫普里亞》；這部詩講的是導致特洛伊戰爭的種種事件。
> The writer of the *Odyssey* seems, in Book iv., to have also known the *Cypria*, which dealt with the events that led up to the Trojan war.

〔U1293 para〕
《奧德賽》卷二十四（35–97 行）暗示作者知道《埃提俄匹斯》。提到門農的那一處（《奧德賽》卷十一 522 行）也是如此。
> Book xxiv. of the *Odyssey* (35-97) suggests a knowledge of the *Æthiopis*. So also does the mention of Memnon (*Od*. xi. 522).

〔U1294 para〕
作者可能知道《小伊里亞德》，這可以從《奧德賽》卷四 271–283 行看出端倪──那裡的海倫似乎已改嫁得伊福玻斯──也可以從卷十一 543–562 行看出來；此外還有卷十一 508、509 行，奧德修斯在那裡說是他把涅俄普托勒摩斯帶到斯庫羅斯去的。奧德修斯扮作探子潛入特洛伊（《奧德賽》卷四 242–256 行），普羅克洛斯也列為《小伊里亞德》的情節之一。因此，我看不出《奧德賽》作者熟悉《小伊里亞德》這件事還有什麼可懷疑的；這部詩篇幅顯然不長，只有四卷。
> Knowledge of the *Little Iliad* may be suspected from *Od*. iv. 271-283, where Helen seems to be now married to Deiphobus, and from xi. 543-562; as also from xi. 508, 509, where Ulysses says that he took Neoptolemus to Scyrus. Ulysses entering Troy as a spy (*Od*. iv. 242-256) is also given by Proclus as one of the incidents in the *Little Iliad*. I do not see, therefore, that there can be much doubt about the writer of the *Odyssey* having been acquainted with the *Little Iliad*, a poem which was apparently of no great length, being only in four Books.

〔U1295 para〕
特洛伊人圍著木馬開會議論的那一段（《奧德賽》卷八 492–517 行），則出自兩卷本的《伊利昂陷落記》。
> From the two Books of the *Sack of Troy* we get the account of the council held by the Trojans over the wooden horse (*Od*. viii. 492-517).

〔U1296 para〕
我們已經看到，《奧德賽》的女作者對《伊里亞德》有多熟悉；那麼八部特洛伊詩篇當中，她似乎不知道的只剩一部──我指的是《忒勒戈尼亞》。一般都把這部詩的年代定在《奧德賽》之後，而且照理說這也是對的；但即使它比《奧德賽》早，我們也可以確定《奧德賽》的作者會對它視而不見，因為這部詩幾乎不可能支持她替潘妮洛碧塑造的那種品格。
> We have seen how familiar the authoress of the *Odyssey* was with the *Iliad*; there only remains, therefore, one of the eight Trojan poems which she does not appear to have known—I mean the *Telegony*, which is generally, and one would say correctly, placed later than the *Odyssey*; but even though it were earlier we may be sure that the writer of the *Odyssey* would have ignored it, for it will hardly bear her out in the character she has given of Penelope.

〔U1297 para〕
順帶一提：荷馬（當然是指《伊里亞德》的作者）雖然偶爾說出一些令人聯想到《庫普里亞》的話，但對普羅克洛斯列為特洛伊史詩集成其他各部主題的那些事件，他卻沒有一行詩哪怕只是暗示知道其中任何一件；因此推論起來，除了《庫普里亞》或許──而且很不確定──以外，那些詩在他寫作之前似乎一部也還沒有問世。不過，我們也不能確定事情就是如此。
> In passing I may say that though Homer (meaning, of course, the writer of the *Iliad*) occasionally says things that suggest the Cypria, there is not a line that even suggests knowledge of a single one of the incidents given by Proclus as forming the subjects of the other Books of the Trojan cycle; the inference, therefore, would seem to be that none of them, except possibly, though very uncertainly, the Cypria, had appeared before he wrote. Nevertheless we cannot be sure that this was so.

〔U1298 para〕
這就引出一個奇特的問題：《奧德賽》的作者一方面大量取用《伊里亞德》，另一方面對普羅克洛斯所列、特洛伊史詩集成其他詩篇講過的幾乎所有事件，也毫不避諱地或詳或略加以處理，那她為什麼偏偏避開《伊里亞德》裡的每一件事，一件也不提？
> The curious question now arises why the writer of the *Odyssey* should have avoided referring to a single Iliadic incident, while showing no unwillingness to treat more or less fully of almost all those mentioned by Proclus as dealt with in the other poems of the Trojan cycle, and also while laying the *Iliad* under such frequent contributions.

〔U1299 para〕
我記得曾經對一位大出版商說，某本書顯然大大受惠於另一本書，卻對那本書隻字未提。那位出版商問：「這位作者有沒有提到其他講同一題目的現代著作？」我回答：「當然有。」他說：「那我告訴你，我們幾乎一成不變的經驗是：一個作者列舉了好些別的書，卻漏掉一本他明明借用過的，那麼被漏掉的那本，正是給他啟發最多的那一本。」他這番話似乎解開了我的困惑：為什麼《奧德賽》的作者對《伊里亞德》的情節如此敬而遠之。那正是她想要與之匹敵──甚至想要取而代之──的詩。她知道那是特洛伊史詩集成中最出色的一部，而且熟到書裡合用的詩句不斷自己湧上心頭──合用的詩句若不拿來挪用，又有什麼用處？她知道自己應付其他詩篇綽綽有餘，對《伊里亞德》卻沒有那麼篤定，所以凡是《伊里亞德》已經佔據的地盤，她一寸也不肯踏進去。
> I remember saying to a great publisher that a certain book was obviously much indebted to a certain other book to which no reference was made. "Has the writer," said the publisher in question, "referred to other modern books on the same subject?" I answered, "Certainly." "Then," said he, "let me tell you that it is our almost unvaried experience that when a writer mentions a number of other books, and omits one which he has evidently borrowed from, the omitted book is the one which has most largely suggested his own." His words seemed to explain my difficulty about the way in which the writer of the *Odyssey* lets the incidents of the *Iliad* so severely alone. It was the poem she was trying to rival, if not to supersede. She knew it to be far the finest of the Trojan cycle; she was so familiar with it that appropriate lines from it were continually suggesting themselves to her—and what is an appropriate line good for if it is not to be appropriated? She knew she could hold her own against the other poems, but she did not feel so sure about the *Iliad*, and she would not cover any of the ground which it had already occupied.

〔U1300 para〕
當然，總還有另一種可能的解釋：《奧德賽》的作者也許聽過一些有關荷馬私生活的傳聞，心裡很不以為然。他也許打過老婆，或者拐走了別人的老婆，或者兩樣都幹過，又或者做了上百件別的事，讓他不完全是阿瑞忒會樂意讓女兒多加理睬的那種人──除非萬不得已。不過我相信，上一段的解釋最合情理。
> Of course there is always this other explanation possible, I mean that traditions about Homer's private life may have been known to the writer of the *Odyssey*, which displeased her. He may have beaten his wife, or run away with somebody else's, or both, or done a hundred things which made him not exactly the kind of person whom Arete would like her daughter to countenance more than was absolutely necessary. I believe, however, that the explanation given in the preceding paragraph is the most reasonable.

〔U1301 para〕
現在讓我說明，在我看來《奧德賽》在這位女詩人手中是怎麼發展起來的。我不認為她一開始就存心要寫一部篇幅浩大的史詩。這部作品似乎是從小小的開頭在她手中一點一點長出來的，每多寫一次，就替更進一步的發展開了一扇門，直到最後《奧德賽》就這麼成形了──與其說是精心觀察經營出來的東西，不如說是自然而然長成的。假如它是經營出來的，無疑會少掉許多反常、矛盾、荒謬和小小的疏漏──任何一部事先沒有把通盤計畫想清楚的長篇作品，在發展過程中都免不了這些毛病。可是，少了這些，它肯定也會失去不少魅力。
> And now let me explain what I consider to have been the development of the *Odyssey* in the hands of the poetess. I cannot think that she deliberately set herself to write an epic poem of great length. The work appears to have grown on her hands piecemeal from small beginnings, each additional effort opening the door for further development, till at last there the *Odyssey* was—a spontaneous growth rather than a thing done by observation. Had it come by observation, no doubt it would have been freer from the anomalies, inconsistencies, absurdities, and small slovenlinesses which are inseparable from the development of any long work, the plan of which has not been fully thought out beforehand. But surely in losing these it would have lost not a little of its charm.

〔U1303 quote〕
但在這種形式的統一之下，仍可察覺到一個過程的痕跡：不同的作品曾經被拿來彼此調適。
> But under this unity of form there are perceptible traces of a process by which different compositions were adapted to one another.

〔U1304 para〕
在前一頁的一條註裡，他告訴我們，基希霍夫認為卷一最前面的 87 行，原是原始的《奧德修斯歸鄉》的開篇引子。
> In a note on the preceding page he tells us that Kirchhoff regards the first 87 verses of Book i. as having formed the exordium of the original Return of Ulysses.

〔U1305 para〕
我自己的結論──就我所記得，是在讀到傑布教授《荷馬導論》的任何一個字之前得出的──和上述看法大部分相合。我發現《奧德賽》是由兩首各自獨立、目標迥然不同的詩合成一部作品；合得並非不巧妙，但還沒有巧妙到能掩飾計畫改變的地步。這兩首詩是：一、奧德修斯造訪費埃克斯人，以及他親口講述的歷險故事。二、潘妮洛碧與求婚者的故事，附帶忒勒馬科斯航往皮洛斯的插曲。兩者之中，第一首寫成的時候，作者還根本沒有打算處理第二首；而到了最後，第二首卻變得比第一首更重要。
> My own conclusions, arrived at to the best of my belief before I had read a word of Professor Jebb's *Introduction*, agree in great part with the foregoing. I found the *Odyssey* to consist of two distinct poems, with widely different aims, and united into a single work, not unskilfully, but still not so skilfully as to conceal a change of scheme. The two poems are: 1. The visit of Ulysses to the Phæacians, with the story of his adventures as related by himself. 2. The story of Penelope and the suitors, with the episode of Telemachus's voyage to Pylos. Of these two, the first was written before the writer had any intention of dealing with the second, while the second in the end became more important than the first.

It is a perfect introduction to the Return of Ulysses, but it is no fit opening for the *Odyssey* as it stands. I had better perhaps give it more fully than I have done in my abridgement. It runs:—

〔U1307 quote〕

Tell me too about all these things, oh daughter of Jove, from whatever source you may know them (i. 1-10).

〔U1312 para〕
從這裡開始，詩中提到求婚者與潘妮洛碧的地方，只有一處確定、另一處存疑，一直到（照基希霍夫的說法）卷十三 184 行為止。我原本以為兩首詩的接合點在第 187 行的中間，而那行後半的 ἔγρετο（他醒來）原本或許是 εὖδεν（他睡著）；但接合點一定就在這一行附近某處，而現在我既已明白為什麼要讓奧德修斯在離開斯刻里亞時睡得那麼沉，我十分願意採納基希霍夫的意見。
> From this point the poem continues with only one certain, and another doubtful, reference to the suitors and Penelope, until (according to Kirchhoff) line 184 of Book xiii. I had thought that the point of juncture between the two poems was in the middle of line 187, and that the ἔγρετο in the second half of the line had perhaps been originally εὖδεν; but it must be somewhere close about this line, and I am quite ready to adopt Kirchhoff's opinion now that I have come to see why Ulysses was made to sleep so profoundly on leaving Scheria.

> She could not ignore the suitors altogether; their existence and Penelope's profligacy were too notorious. She could not make Ulysses and Penelope meet happily while the suitors were still in his house; and even though he killed them, he could never condone Penelope's conduct—not as an epic hero. The writer of the *Odyssey* had evidently thought that she could find some way out of the difficulty, but when it came to the point she discovered that she must either make Ulysses kill his wife along with the suitors, or contend that from first to last she had been pure as new fallen snow. She chose the second alternative, as she would be sure to do, and brazened it out with her audience as best she could. At line 187, therefore, of Book xiii. or thereabouts, she broke up her 'Return' camp and started on a new campaign.

In the speech of Jove at the council of the gods (i. 32-43), he is not thinking about the suitors, as he would assuredly do if the writer had as yet meant to introduce them. > Noting, therefore, that omission is a more telling indication of scheme than lines which, when a new subject is being grafted on to an old one, are certain to be inserted where necessary in order to unify the work, I have no hesitation in believing that Books i. 1-79 and v. 28—xiii. 187 or thereabouts, formed as much as the authoress ever wrote of the original poem; I have the less hesitation in adopting this conclusion because, though I believe that I came to it independently as any one must do who studies the *Odyssey* with due attention, I find myself in substantial agreement with Kirchhoff in spite of much difference of detail, for I cannot admit that the two poems are by two or more separate people.

> The introduction of lines xi. 115-137 and of line ix. 535, with a writing of a new Council of the gods at the beginning of Book v. to take the place of the one that was removed to Book i. 1-79, were the only things that were done to give even a semblance of unity to the old scheme and the new, and to conceal the fact that the Muse after being asked to sing of one subject spends two thirds of her time in singing a very different one, with a climax for which no one had asked her. For, roughly, the Return occupies eight Books and Penelope and the suitors sixteen.

卷十一 115–137 行在寫卷十三的時候還不存在，這一點可以由下面這件事證明：奧德修斯對費埃克斯人說，他希望回家時看到妻子和親友一起平安度日（卷十三 42、43 行）。假如忒瑞西阿斯早已告訴他，他家裡會擠滿正在吞吃他家產的仇敵，而他非得把他們殺掉不可，他就不可能說出這種話。他已經發現忒瑞西阿斯對太陽神赫利俄斯的牛群的預言完全應驗，就幾乎不可能把這樣一個預言忘掉。事實上，他還把自己造訪冥府、會見忒瑞西阿斯的事告訴了潘妮洛碧（卷二十三 323 行），可見他是記得的。同樣明顯的是（從卷十三 382 行起），奧德修斯那時是頭一次從雅典娜那裡得知求婚者的事──假如忒瑞西阿斯的預言早已寫好，就不可能如此。
> That lines xi. 115-137 were non-existent when Book xiii. was being written is demonstrated by the fact of Ulysses' saying to the Phæacians that he hoped he should find his wife living with her friends in peace (xiii. 42, 43). He could not have said this if Teiresias had already told him that his house would be full of enemies who were eating up his estate, and whom he would have to kill. He could hardly forget such a prophecy after having found Teiresias quite correct about the cattle of the Sun-god. Indeed he tells Penelope about his visit to Hades and his interview with Teiresias (xxiii. 323), so it is plain he remembered it. It is plain, again (from xiii. 382 &c.), that Ulysses was then learning from Minerva about the suitors for the first time—which could not be if Teiresias' prophecy had been already written.

〔U1323 para〕
說來令人驚訝：明明只要再稍加修改，一切就能完全理順，作者卻甘心在這裡那裡留下一些段落，而她一定知道這些段落會洩露她作品的不夠一致；但我們應該非常感謝她，沒有更仔細地把它收拾整齊。比起她把作品弄得處處四平八穩，我們如今能從中認識她的地方要多得多，而我們珍視的是她本人，甚至勝過她的詩。她顯然寧可修補也不願刪除，這也難怪；因為如果真如極可能的情形那樣，這部作品是用硬化青銅、甚至鋼[註3]製成的尖筆刻在鉛板上的，修改起來就不會像我們今天這麼容易。何況我們大家只要做得到，也都寧可修修補補，而不願整段刪掉。卷四 621–624 行那段插入之所以顯得突兀，很可能──不過我幾乎不必說，這充其量只是一種可能──只是因為插入四行，不必把手稿割得太厲害，而較長的一段就得對手稿動更大的手術。
> It is surprising; seeing what a little further modification would have put everything quite straight, that the writer should have been content to leave passages here and there which she must have known would betray the want of homogeneity in her work, but we should be very thankful to her for not having tidied it up with greater care. We learn far more about her than we should do if she had made her work go more perfectly upon all fours, and it is herself that we value even more than her poem. She evidently preferred cobbling to cancelling, and small wonder, for if, as was very probably the case, the work was traced with a sharply pointed style of hardened bronze, or even steel,[註3] on plates of lead, alteration would not be so easy as it is with us. Besides, we all cobble rather than cancel if we can. It is quite possible, but I need hardly say that it is not more than a mere possibility, that the abruptness of the interpolation in Book iv. lines 621-624, may be due simply to its having been possible to introduce four lines without cutting the MS. about very badly, when a longer passage would have necessitated a more radical interference with it.

那麼，我們要在卷一 1–79 行與卷五 28 行至卷十三 187 行前後，或者更粗略地說，在卷五至卷十二（含）之中，去尋找這首詩的起點。這幾卷除了上面提到、加在忒瑞西阿斯預言上的那二十行之外，彼此並無矛盾；但它們的範圍並不一致，儘管在風格與處理手法上是一致的。它們自然分成兩組，每組四卷，也就是卷五至卷八與卷九至卷十二。第一組的寫作目的，是把奧德修斯帶到斯刻里亞，並展示費埃克斯人與作者本人──奧德修斯引起的興趣倒在其次；第二組的寫作目的，則是描述一趟環西西里的航行。
> We look, then, for the inception of the poem in Books i. 1-79 and v. 28-xiii. 187 or thereabouts, or more roughly in Books v.-xii. inclusive. These Books, though they contain no discrepancies among themselves except the twenty lines added to the prophecy of Teiresias above referred to are not homogeneous in scope, though they are so in style and treatment. They split themselves into two groups of four, *i.e*., v.-viii. and ix.-xii. The first group is written to bring Ulysses to Scheria and to exhibit the Phæacians and the writer herself—the interest in Ulysses being subordinate; the second is written to describe a periplus of Sicily.

卷九至卷十二似乎寫在卷五至卷八之前。這一點可以從雅典娜完全缺席推知。作者既然在卷五至卷八裡那樣隨意地讓女神出場，若說她會在故事裡有大好機會讓女神插手的時候，卻任由女神消失不見，那是不可想像的。這幾卷肯定與全詩其餘部分出自同一人之手。它們顯示出同等程度的《伊里亞德》影響；沒有哪個地方比這裡更明白地顯出女性的手筆；也沒有哪個地方比這裡更處處可見西西里，尤其是特拉帕尼的影子，不論是直接的還是間接的。我們之所以確信愛奧尼亞群島是照著埃加迪群島描畫的，正是從卷九開頭得來的；而奧德修斯的航行，如我已經說明過的，實際上是從埃律克斯山開始，到特拉帕尼結束。因此，我們大可完全拋開卷九至卷十二出自另一位作者的想法。
> Books ix.-xii. appear to have been written before Books v.-viii. We may gather this from the total absence of Minerva. These Books are certainly by the same hand as the rest of the poem. 這段插曲或許是詩人結合刻畫人物的才能與諷刺幽默的才能最出色的範例。他的作品中，沒有哪一部分比這段更是充分評價他的天才所不可或缺的；然而或許也沒有哪一部分像這段一樣，這麼少被人理解。只要看看最受推崇的注疏的大意，或許更要看看最通行譯本的本文就知道了：通篇調劑著整段奇遇的那種輕快而帶譏諷的描寫與對話語調，在那裡竟被換成了《伊里亞德》最莊重場面那種悲劇式的肅穆。
> This episode is, perhaps, the most brilliant specimen of the poet's combined talent for the delineation of character and for satirical humour. While there is no portion of his works a right understanding of which is so indispensable to a full estimate of his genius, there is none, perhaps, which has been so little understood. Appeal may be made to the tenor of the most esteemed commentaries, still more, perhaps, to the text of the most popular translations, where the gay sarcastic tone of description and dialogue which seasons the whole adventure, is replaced by the tragic solemnity of the gravest scones of the *Iliad*.

〔U1335 para〕
人只會找到自己帶去的東西。傑出的荷馬學者在《奧德賽》比較詼諧的部分找到那麼多嚴肅，會不會正是因為他們自己把嚴肅帶了進去？在嚴肅的人眼裡，萬事都是嚴肅的。我從戈蘭茨先生《坦普爾版莎士比亞》書末的註釋得知，柯立芝看不出《哈姆雷特》裡穿插的那些伶人台詞有什麼戲仿的意味。他說：──
> People find what they bring. Is it possible that eminent Homeric scholars have found so much seriousness in the more humorous parts of the *Odyssey* because they brought it there? To the serious all things are serious. Coleridge, so I learn from the notes at the end of Mr. Gollancz's *Temple Shakespeare*, saw no burlesque in the speeches of the players which are introduced into *Hamlet*. He says:—

〔U1336 quote〕
以為這裡有意戲仿，這種想法根本不值一駁；這些詩句作為史詩敘事，堪稱絕妙。
> The fancy that a burlesque was intended sinks below criticism; the lines, as epic narrative, are superb.

〔U1337 para〕
既然戈蘭茨先生沒有註明出處，我也只好不註。戈蘭茨先生接著說，柯立芝假如讀過《蒂朵與埃涅阿斯》第二幕第一場──那是馬羅未完成的一齣戲──他就會改變看法；但我不相信他會改。
> As Mr. Gollancz has given no reference, so neither can I. Mr. Gollancz continues that if Coleridge had read Act II. Scene i. of *Dido* and *Æneas*—a play left unfinished by Marlowe—he would have changed his mind, but I do not believe he would.

〔U1338 para〕
話雖如此，我認為作者是一半說笑、一半認真，有時候連她自己恐怕也很難說清楚，究竟是說笑的成分多，還是認真的成分多。同樣地，知道清唱劇《納西瑟斯》（本書書末登有它的廣告）的人會承認：有些人完全明白，世上沒有任何音樂比得上韓德爾的音樂那樣偉大，卻仍然要嘗試以韓德爾的風格作曲；等他們寫完了，幾乎說不清自己是說笑多些還是認真多些，雖然在寫的時候，他們完全相信自己不過是盡己所能，寫出韓德爾若晚生一百年左右、為這樣的歌詞譜曲時會寫的那種音樂。
> At the same time I take it that the writer was one half laughing and the other half serious, and would sometimes have been hard put to it to know whether she was more in the one vein than in the other. So those who know the cantata *Narcissus*, advertised at the end of this volume, will admit that there are people who are fully aware that there is no music in this world so great as Handel's, but who will still try to write music in the style of Handel, and when they have done it, hardly know whether they have been more in jest or earnest, though while doing it they fully believed that they were only writing, so far as in them lay, the kind of music which Handel would have written for such words had he lived a hundred years or so later than he did.

〔U1339 para〕
我們可以注意到──雖然無法據此推斷出這首詩各部分寫成的年代──在《奧德賽》的前四卷裡，季節似乎是夏天而非冬天。在其他各卷裡（當然不算奧德修斯講述自己經歷的那幾卷），季節無疑是冬天，或者是初春時節。同樣值得注意的是，雪在《伊里亞德》裡一再出現，荷馬顯然深深感受到雪的美，但在《奧德賽》裡卻沒有出現，幾乎連提都沒提到。我或許應該告訴某些讀者：特洛阿斯的冬天又長又嚴酷，而西西里西岸幾乎從不下雪，冬天甚至比阿爾及爾還要溫和。
> We may note, without, however, being able to deduce anything from it as regards the dates at which the various parts of the poem were composed, that in the first four Books of the *Odyssey* the season appears to be summer rather than winter. In all the other Books (of course excluding those in which Ulysses tells his story) the season is unquestionably winter, or very early spring. It is noticeable also that snow, which appears so repeatedly in the *Iliad*, and of which Homer evidently felt the beauty very strongly, does not appear, and is hardly even mentioned, in the *Odyssey*. I should perhaps tell some readers that winter is long and severe in the Troad, while on the West coast of Sicily snow is almost unknown, and the winter is even milder than that of Algiers.

〔U1340 para〕
我或許也不該略過這個事實：琥珀在《伊里亞德》裡從未提及，在《奧德賽》裡卻出現了三次。[註4]這也許純屬偶然；不過西西里是個出產琥珀的地方，事實上至今仍是；恩納古城，也就是今天的卡斯特羅焦萬尼，那裡的博物館藏有大批西西里琥珀；我還從可靠的來源聽說──只是我沒有查證告訴我的人所言是否屬實──南肯辛頓博物館也能見到一些精美的標本。《大英百科全書》談到西西里琥珀時說：──
> I ought also perhaps hardly to pass over the fact that amber, which is never mentioned in the *Iliad*, appears three times in the *Odyssey*.[註4] This may be mere accident, nevertheless Sicily was an amber-producing country, and indeed still is so; a large collection of Sicilian amber exists in the museum of Castrogiovanni, the ancient Enna, and I have been assured on good authority, but have not verified my informant's statement, that some fine specimens may be seen in the South Kensington Museum. Speaking of Sicilian amber the *Encyclopedia Britannica* says:—

〔U1341 quote〕
最美的標本，或許要算在卡塔尼亞發現的那些。它們常常閃現一種美麗的紫色光澤，這是其他產地的琥珀所看不到的。
> The most beautiful specimens are, perhaps, those which are found at Catania. They often possess a beautiful play of purple not to be observed in the product of other places.

〔U1342 para〕
我弄不清楚前四卷是寫在後十二卷之前還是之後；大概是先寫的，但反面也有一些理由可說。我不打算去解決這個問題，只想再補充一點：只要我們記得，《奧德賽》的兩大部分──費埃克斯插曲連同奧德修斯的歸鄉，以及潘妮洛碧與求婚者的故事──都顯示出明白無誤的跡象，表明它們寫於同一地點，出自女性之手，而且出自一位顯然還非常年輕的女性之手，又記得在這兩部分之間，找不出一絲一毫格律、風格或語言習慣上的差異，那麼我們應當得出的唯一結論就是：這首詩從第一頁到最後一頁，都出自同一位女性之手。我想，在沒有任何相反證據的情況下──因為確實沒有任何稱得上證據的證據──我們也可以斷定，我們手上這首詩，實際上就是它在女作者手中定稿時的樣子。
> I cannot make out whether the first four Books were written before the last twelve or after; probably they were written first, but there is something to be said also on the other side. I will not attempt to settle this point, and will only add that when we hear in mind how both the two main divisions of the *Odyssey*—the Phæacian episode with the Return of Ulysses, and the story of Penelope and the suitors, show unmistakeable signs of having been written at one place, by woman, by woman who is evidently still very young, and that not a trace of difference in versification, style, or idiom can be found between the two divisions, the only conclusion we should come to is that the poem was written by one and the same woman from the first page to the last. I think we may also conclude in the absence of all evidence to the contrary—for assuredly none exists that deserves the name of evidence—that we have the poem to all intents and purposes in the shape which it had assumed in the hands of the authoress.
