〔U1009 heading〕
第十章
> CHAPTER X.

〔U1010 para〕
關於奧德修斯航程的進一步細節，以證實其航程主要是一趟以埃律克斯山與特拉帕尼為起點和終點的環西西里航行
> FURTHER DETAILS REGARDING THE VOYAGES OF ULYSSES, TO CONFIRM THE VIEW THAT THEY WERE MAINLY A SAIL ROUND SICILY, BEGINNING AND ENDING WITH MT. ERYX AND TRAPANI.

〔U1011 para〕
前一章所說的，應足以確立奧德修斯所走的航線就是我地圖上標示的那一條；不過關於庫克羅普斯人、埃俄羅斯島周圍的城牆、塞壬、漂移之岩，以及與航程有關的其他事項，我還有些話要說；為了讓整體輪廓更寬廣、更簡明，我先把它們保留下來。
> What I have said in the preceding chapter should be enough to establish that the course taken by Ulysses was the one indicated in my map, but I have remarks to make on the Cyclopes, the wall round the island of Æolus, the Sirens, the Wandering Cliffs, and other matters connected with the voyages which I have reserved in order to keep the general view more broad and simple.

〔U1012 para〕
庫克羅普斯人住在埃律克斯山，這是我最有責任證實的一點，因為只要這一點獲得承認，再把斯庫拉與卡律布狄斯以及斯刻里亞都當作已經找到，其他地點便都會自然而然地落到我為它們標定的位置上，我不擔心會有什麼爭議。那麼，讓我們轉向法維尼亞納，姑且接受它就是奧德修斯獵山羊的那座島。
> The habitat of the Cyclopes on Mt. Eryx is the point which it is most incumbent on me to establish, for if this be conceded, and both Scylla and Charybdis, and Scheria be taken as found, all the other places fall so spontaneously on to the sites I have marked for them, that I fear no dispute concerning them. Let us turn, then, to Favognana and accept it for the moment as the island on which Ulysses hunted the goats.

〔U1013 para〕
我不禁納悶：作者為什麼那麼用心地召來一片濃重的黑暗，寫得那樣堂皇而詳盡，還藉神明之助把奧德修斯領進這座島的港口，而不讓他四處張望，看看那片「並不很遠」的陸地呢？
> Why, I wonder, was the author so careful to invoke a thick darkness, so pompous and circumstantial, and to pilot Ulysses into the harbour of this island by divine assistance, rather than permit him to look about him and see the land, which was "not very far" off.

〔U1014 para〕
答案其實「並不很遠」，一找就到。如果奧德修斯從食蓮人之地駛近時看到了西西里本土，他一定會沿著海岸一路走下去，那樣他就會被直接帶進特拉帕尼港。而雖然所有聽眾都知道，作者早已把特拉帕尼處理成奧德修斯整個航程的最後一站，奧德修斯本人卻不該在按部就班抵達之前，就對它有所知曉。
> The answer is "not very far" to seek. If Ulysses had seen the main land of Sicily as he approached it from that of the Lotus-eaters, he would have been sure to have followed it up, and in this case he would have been taken straight into Trapani harbour. Now, though the writer, as all the audience would know, had already dealt with Trapani, as the last point in all Ulysses' voyages, Ulysses himself ought not to know anything about it till he comes to it in due course.

〔U1015 para〕
波呂斐摩斯的洞穴──農民至今仍稱之為 *la grotta di Polifemo*（波呂斐摩斯洞）──位於特拉帕尼以北約六七英里處；奧德修斯必須被帶到那裡，而且可能的話，不能讓他看到一座八年後他將認得是斯刻里亞的城市，以免攪亂他自己或聽眾的心思。藉著一點霧氣，可以勉強設想他從法維尼亞納島前往皮佐隆戈岬與波呂斐摩斯的洞穴，而沒看見費埃克斯人的城市──只要他不特別使勁往那個方向看；但如果讓他一路駛到斯刻里亞的港口，即使是奧德修斯，也不得不注意到它。因此，最好讓某位神明把他帶到那座島，而完全不讓他看到任何其他陸地；這就是作者如此用心描寫那片濃黑的緣故。我們將會看到，後來（就故事設定的時間而言；在她詩的結構上則較早）她在完全相同的地方、出於相同的理由，又召來一片黑暗，讓奧德修斯完全無法判斷自己身在何處（卷五 291–294 行）──因為在那裡，同樣必須讓奧德修斯從特拉帕尼以南某處，到達城北另一處，而不看見這座城鎮。
> The cave of Polyphemus—still called among the peasants *la grotta di Polifemo*—was some six or seven miles North of Trapani; Ulysses had got to be taken there, and if possible, without unsettling either his own mind or that of the audience by showing him a city which eight years later he was to know as Scheria. He could, with the help of a little mist, be just supposed to go from the island of Favognana to the promontory of Pizzolungo and the cave of Polyphemus, without seeing the city of the Phæacians if he did not look particularly hard in that direction, but even Ulysses would have been compelled to take note of Scheria if he had been allowed to go on till he reached its harbour. It was better, therefore, that some god should take him to the island without letting him see any other land at all, and hence the intense darkness which the writer has been so careful to describe. We shall see that later on (as regards the supposed time, though earlier in the structure of her poem) she invokes a darkness which makes it impossible for Ulysses to form any idea of his whereabouts, in exactly the same place, and for the same reasons (v. 291-294)—for here too it is necessary to get Ulysses from a point South of Trapani, to another on the North side of it without seeing the town.

〔U1016 para〕
把我那張埃加迪群島地圖（第 177 頁）和特拉帕尼與埃律克斯山地圖（第 164 頁）合起來看，就能看出奧德修斯從法維尼亞納到 *Grotta di Polifemo*（波呂斐摩斯洞）會走的路線──這是特拉帕尼附近最大的洞穴，比其他洞穴大得多，至今仍用來在夜裡圈養一大群綿羊。波呂斐摩斯擲出的兩塊巨石，應當看作：第一塊是阿西內利[註1]，第二塊是名為福爾米凱的兩座小島，由於兩島緊挨在一起，算作一塊。
> My map of the Ægadean islands (p. 177) combined with that of Trapani and Mt. Eryx (p. 164) will show the course Ulysses would make from Favognana to the *Grotta di Polifemo*—which is [by] far the largest cave near Trapani, and is still used as a place in which to keep a large flock of sheep by night. The two rocks which Polyphemus threw should be seen, the first as the Asinelli,[註1] and the second as the two small islands called Formiche, which, being close together, are taken as one.

〔U1017 para〕
因此，我在防止奧德修斯看見特拉帕尼的這份用心中，找到了一個相當有力的論據，支持這樣的看法：法維尼亞納就是奧德修斯獵山羊的島，而波呂斐摩斯的洞穴位於埃律克斯山。
> I find, therefore, in the care taken to prevent Ulysses from seeing Trapani, a considerable argument for the belief that Favognana was the island where Ulysses hunted the goats, and that the cave of Polyphemus was on Mt. Eryx.

〔U1018 para〕
另有一項跡象，雖然力道不大，似乎也暗示庫克羅普斯人當時仍是費埃克斯人的近鄰。
> Another indication, though one of no great strength, seems to suggest that the Cyclopes were still near neighbours of the Phæacians.

〔U1019 para〕
在卷六開頭，我們得知費埃克斯人過去住在一個叫許珀瑞亞的地方，「靠近無法無天的庫克羅普斯人」，但近年已遷到斯刻里亞──正如我說過的，斯刻里亞的意思是「日德蘭」。在一段我的縮譯沒有收入的文字中，阿爾基諾俄斯隨口說道（卷七 205、206 行），費埃克斯人與眾神的親緣，就和庫克羅普斯人及巨人族一樣近。且不說阿爾基諾俄斯身為波賽頓的孫子，算是波呂斐摩斯的半個姪子；庫克羅普斯人如此自然地浮上他的心頭，表示他們雖然不像從前那麼近，大概仍是他最近的鄰居。
> At the beginning of Book vi. we learn that the Phæacians used to live at a place called Hypereia, "near the lawless Cyclopes," but had of late years been moved to Scheria, which, as I have said, means Jutland. In a passage which I have not given in my abridgement Alcinous says casually (vii. 205, 206) that the Phæacians are as closely related to the gods as the Cyclopes and the giants are. Passing over the fact that Alcinous, being grandson to Neptune, was half nephew to Polyphemus, the spontaneousness with which the Cyclopes rise to his mind suggests that though less near than they had been, they were still about the nearest neighbours that he had.

〔U1020 para〕
巨人族不過是庫克羅普斯人換個說法，無疑就是建造埃律克斯那些雄偉巨石城牆的人的後裔。許珀瑞亞，即「上城」，大概位於埃律克斯山頂的東端；那裡至今仍可追尋到一道極古老的城牆，性質與山西端西卡尼城市的城牆完全不同。這道城牆的遺跡就在 *Ruccazzù dei Corvi*（烏鴉岩）上方，位於佩波利伯爵的園地內，最初是伯爵本人指給我看的。外地人除非由見過的人帶路，否則不太可能找得到。
> The giants are only the Cyclopes over again, and are doubtless the descendants of the people who built the noble megalithic walls of Eryx. Hypereia, or Upper-town, was probably at the Eastern end of the top of Mt. Eryx on a site where a very ancient wall, of totally different character to those of the Sican city at the West end of the mountain, may yet be traced. The remains of this wall are just above the *Ruccazzù dei Corvi*, in Count Pepoli's grounds, and were first shown me by the Count. A stranger is little likely to find them unless conducted by one who has seen them.

〔U1021 para〕
關於許珀瑞亞，我要重申：西西里境內的地名，除了一個部分的例外之外，全是虛構的，連特里納克里亞也是──修昔底德告訴我們，那是西西里最古老的名字──它變成了「特里納基亞」島，即「三尖」島；然而一出了西西里，地名便都是真實的。這讓人有理由認為，作者描寫的是真實的人物與真實的地方，只是用一層她知道聽眾一眼就能看穿的薄薄偽裝，把他們改頭換面。面具只摘下過一次，而且只有片刻：奧德修斯說他剛從西卡尼亞來（卷二十四 307 行）；但這不算數，因為奧德修斯這時被設定是在說謊。
> As regards Hypereia I would repeat that all the names of places in Sicily with one partial exception are fictitious, even Trinacria, which Thucydides tells us was the most ancient name of Sicily, becoming "the Thrinacian," or "three-pointed," island; whereas as soon as we are outside Sicily the names are real. This affords ground for thinking that the writer was drawing real people as well as real places, and travestying them under flimsy disguises that she knew her audience would see through. Once only is the mask dropped for a moment, when Ulysses says that he had just come from Sicania (xxiv. 307), but this does not count, for Ulysses is supposed to be lying.

〔U1022 para〕
舉例來說，庫克羅普斯人（Cyclopes）這個名字，也就是「圓臉」──這個字本身看不出還有任何別的意思，而我從利德爾與斯科特的辭典中看到，巴門尼德把月亮稱為 Cyclops──不過是作者取的綽號。如果 μήλωψ 意為「蘋果臉」，κύκλωψ 就該是「圓圈臉」。這個字本身沒有任何暗示，《奧德賽》裡也沒有任何暗示，說庫克羅普斯人是額頭中央只有一隻圓眼的民族。這樣顯著的特徵，不可能不言自明[註2]；而在最早的希臘藝術家眼中，它也並非不言自明，從他們總是給波呂斐摩斯畫兩隻眼睛就看得出來。要到羅馬時代，他才變成獨眼；而《奧德賽》給他的眉毛是複數（卷九 389 行），這就意味著眼睛也是複數。誠然，作者只弄瞎了一隻眼睛，但她可以指望如此嚴重的傷勢在另一隻眼睛引起交感性發炎，並認為燒烤了其中一隻，就足以讓兩隻都瞎掉。之所以只弄瞎一隻眼睛，不是因為波呂斐摩斯沒有兩隻眼，而是因為他那根木樁沒有兩個尖頭；作者既想不出怎麼把一件分叉的工具弄進洞裡，也想不出在這麼多人已被吃掉之後，剩下的人要怎麼揮舞它。
> The name Cyclopes, for example, or "round faces"—for there is nothing in the word to show that it means anything else than this, and I see from Liddell & Scott that Parmenides calls the moon Cyclops—is merely an author's nick name. If μήλωψ means "apple-faced," κύκλωψ should mean "circle-faced." As there is nothing in the word, so neither is there in the *Odyssey*, to suggest that the Cyclopes were a people with only one round eye in the middle of their foreheads. Such a marked feature does not go without saying,[註2] and that it did not go with the earliest Greek artists appears from the fact that they always gave Polyphemus two eyes. It is not till Roman times that he becomes monophthalmic, and the *Odyssey* gives him eyebrows in the plural (ix. 389), which involve eyes in the plural also. True, the writer only blinds one eye, but she could trust to the sympathetic inflammation which so serious an injury would excite in the other eye, and would consider that she had sufficiently blinded both by roasting one of them. One eye alone was blinded, not because Polyphemus had not got two, but because his pole had not got two prongs, and the writer saw neither how to get a bifurcated instrument into the cave, nor how to wield it now that so many of the men had been eaten.

〔U1023 para〕
所以我們可以確定，「庫克羅普斯人」的意思不過是「月亮臉」。波呂斐摩斯這個名字，在《伊里亞德》中是一位英雄的名字，或許是從那部詩借來，作為當地巨人（如果有的話）的化名。無論他原本叫什麼名字，也無論他是《奧德賽》之前就有的巨人，還是《奧德賽》的作者讓他誕生的，他如今以孔圖拉諾之名存在。我有時會想，這個名字會不會與希臘字 κόντος（長竿）和 οὐρανός（天空）有關，表示這個巨人高到能用他的手杖在天上戳個洞。果真如此，他在《奧德賽》的時代很可能就叫孔圖拉諾，或類似的名字，而奧德修斯弄瞎他所用的，正是他自己名字裡紀念的那根 κόντος。自《奧德賽》寫成以來，這個巨人已長大許多，*grotta di Polifemo*（波呂斐摩斯洞）再大，他也絕對擠不進去；因為他雙腳踩在平原上，肚子撐在埃律克斯山頂，身子前傾，把巨大的雙手伸進博納賈與科法諾之間的海裡捉鮪魚。有人打擾他時，他就從埃律克斯山頂扯下大石塊，朝所有打擾他的人砸去。
> "Cyclopes," therefore, we may be sure, means nothing more than "moon-faced." The name Polyphemus is found as that of a hero in the *Iliad*, and is perhaps a pseudonym for the local giant (if there was one) taken from that poem. "Whatever his name may have been, and whether he was a pre-Odyssean giant, or whether the writer of the *Odyssey* called him into being, he exists now under the name of Conturràno. I have sometimes wondered whether this name may have any connection with the Greek words κόντος and οὐρανός, and may indicate that the giant was so tall as to be able to knock a hole in the sky with his staff. Should this be so, his name, as likely as not was Conturràno, or something near it, in the days of the *Odyssey*, and it was with the κόντος commemorated in his own name that Ulysses blinded him. The giant has grown greatly since the *Odyssey* was written, and large as the *grotta di Polifemo* is, he could never get inside it; for he rests his feet on the plain while he props his stomach on the top of Mt. Eryx, and bending forward plunges his huge hands into the sea between Bonagia and Cofàno, to catch tunnies. When disturbed he tears great rocks from the top of Mt. Eryx, and dashes them at all who interrupt him.

〔U1024 para〕
重複並總結一下──這一點我不再多辯了：我認為庫克羅普斯人是埃律克斯山古老西卡尼居民被征服後的殘餘。他們之所以身材巨大，是因為他們在埃律克斯山上築城所用的石塊巨大無比。這些石塊幾乎看不出用硬化青銅或鐵製工具加工過的痕跡，只有腓尼基人局部重建城牆時，可能在這裡那裡修整過；腓尼基人重建時，部分是從頭（*de novo*）砌起，所用的石塊有些刻著腓尼基文字的採石工標記。[註3]古老的西卡尼工程有很大一部分被保留了下來，屬於真正的巨石時代，那時搬運比切割便宜；後來的世代沒有考慮到改良的切割方法帶來了怎樣的革命，便推論說用這麼大的石塊築城的人必定是大個子，其實他們只是精打細算的人。
> To repeat and to sum up, for I will argue this point no further; I take the Cyclopes to be the conquered remnant of the old Sican inhabitants of Mt. Eryx. They owe their gigantic stature to the huge size of the stones with which the walls of their city on Mt. Eryx were built. These stones show few or no signs of having been worked with a tool of hardened bronze or iron, save in so far as the Phœnicians may have trimmed them here and there when they rebuilt the walls, in part, *de novo*, with stones some of which bear quarry-men's marks in Phœnician characters.[註3] The old Sican work, a good deal of which has been allowed to stand, belongs to the true megalithic age, when it was cheaper to carry than to cut; later generations, failing to consider the revolution which the introduction of improved methods of cutting had effected, argued that the men who built with such large stones must have been large men, whereas in reality they were only economical men.

〔U1025 para〕
一旦切割變得比搬運便宜，我們在埃律克斯、切法盧，以及塞尼、阿爾皮諾、阿拉特里和南義大利許多其他城市看到的那些笨重巨塊，就過時了；但要等到砌層的種種不規則完全被拋棄、換成我們在敘拉古、塞利努斯、塞傑斯塔神廟以及歷史時期幾乎所有希臘與羅馬建築中所見的那種完美規則，還要經過很長的時間。事實上，我知道許多晚至西元十世紀的建築，砌層仍遠不規則；然而，幾乎就在切割變得較便宜之後，趨勢便是朝向砌層更規則、石塊更小，直到另一種巨石主義興起，與早期建造者的巨石主義截然相反──我指的是炫耀的巨石主義。
> As soon as it became cheaper to cut than to carry, the huge unwieldy blocks that we see at Eryx, at Cefalù, and at Segni, Arpino, Allatri, and many another city in Southern Italy, became obsolete, but it was still long before all irregularity in the courses was abandoned for that perfect regularity which we find at Syracuse, Selinunte, the temple of Segesta, and nearly all the Greek and Roman architecture of historic times. Indeed I know many buildings as late as the tenth century after Christ, in which the courses are far from regular; nevertheless the tendency, almost immediately after cutting had become cheaper, was towards greater regularity of courses and the use of smaller stones, until there arose another megalithicism, of a kind diametrically opposed to that of the earlier builders—I mean the megalithicism of display.

〔U1028 para〕

He does not appear to have seen the island of Favognana, which must have been quite near, and it was perhaps as well that he did not, for he could hardly have failed to recognise it as the one on which he had hunted the goats some eight or nine years previously, and this might have puzzled him.

〔U1050 para〕
不過，雖然允許他看到陸地，卻不能允許他沿著海岸走，否則，正如我已經解釋過的，他就會直接駛進斯刻里亞的港口；而作者特意要他在城的北邊遇見瑙西卡，並且在瑙西卡帶他進城之前，對斯刻里亞一無所知。因此，作者讓波賽頓恰在此刻瞥見他，掀起一場可怕的風暴；海天被雲層遮蔽，黑得如同夜晚（卷五 291–294 行），奧德修斯就這樣被帶到很遠的地方，看來是往北，因為等他被帶得夠遠了，雅典娜便讓北風吹送他兩天兩夜，也就是往南吹，直到他抵達那個可以讓瑙西卡遇見他的港口附近。
> But though he is allowed to see the land he must not be permitted to follow it up, or, as I have explained already, he would have gone straight into the harbour of Scheria, whereas he is particularly wanted to meet Nausicaa on the North side of the town, and to know nothing about Scheria till she brings him to it. Neptune, therefore, is made to catch sight of him at this moment and to raise a frightful hurricane; sea and sky become obscured in clouds, with a darkness as dense as night (v. 291-294), and thus Ulysses is carried a long distance apparently to the North, for when he has been taken far enough, Minerva blows him two days and two nights before a North wind, and hence Southwards, till he reaches the harbour near which Nausicaa can meet him.

〔U1051 para〕
《奧德賽》裡再沒有其他如此引人注意的黑暗，除了這一次和卷九 144 行那一次（第 188 頁已提過）。兩者發生在同一地方，出於同一理由──把斯刻里亞城保留到後面。
> There are no other such noticeable darknesses in the *Odyssey*, as this and the one of Book ix. 144, alluded to on p. 188. They both occur in the same place, and for the same reason—to keep the town of Scheria in reserve.

〔U1052 para〕
現在我已經證明，《奧德賽》中所有伊薩卡的場景，都極其忠實地取材自特拉帕尼及其近郊，斯刻里亞的場景也全都如此；此外，我也證明了愛奧尼亞群島其實取材自特拉帕尼外海的埃加迪群島；最後，我證明了奧德修斯的航程實際上始於特拉帕尼，又終於特拉帕尼。至於皮洛斯與拉刻代蒙，我只需指出它們遠在作者與聽眾的知識範圍之外，不必多談。
> I have now shown that all the Ithacan scenes of the *Odyssey* are drawn with singular fidelity from Trapani and its neighbourhood, as also all the Scherian; moreover, I have shown that the Ionian islands are in reality drawn from the Ægadean group off Trapani; lastly I have shown that the voyage of Ulysses in effect begins with Trapani and ends with Trapani again. I need not deal with Pylos and Lacedæmon beyond showing that they were far removed from the knowledge of either writer or audience.

〔U1053 para〕
兩處都沒有提到任何一項自然地貌。忒勒馬科斯與佩西斯特拉托斯乘一輛雙馬戰車，從費拉伊到拉刻代蒙，越過高峻、至今仍無道路的塔宇革托斯山脈──這段不可能的旅程，作者寫來毫無不安。她完全沒有暗示有山要翻越──由此我們可以推斷：要嘛她對皮洛斯與拉刻代蒙之間的地方一無所知，要嘛至少她的聽眾一無所知。不過，或許值得一提的是，雅典娜為了把忒勒馬科斯從伊薩卡送到皮洛斯而提供的西風，更適合把他從西西里送去。如果他是從真正的伊薩卡出發，北風會對他更有利；但雅典娜行事如此古怪，這一點我不打算深究。
> There is not a single natural feature mentioned in either case. The impossible journey of Telemachus and Pisistratus from Pheræ to Lacedæmon in a chariot and pair over the lofty, and even now roadless, range of Mt. Taygetus, causes no uneasiness to the writer. She gives no hint of any mountain to be crossed—from which we may infer, either that she knew nothing of the country between Pylos and Lacedæmon, or that at any rate her audience would not do so. It may, however, be remarked that the West wind which Minerva provided in order to take Telemachus from Ithaca to Pylos, was more suitable for taking him from Sicily. A North wind would have been better for him if he had been coming from the real Ithaca, but Minerva manages things so strangely that I would not press this point.
