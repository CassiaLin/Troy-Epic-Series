〔U0078 heading〕
第一章
> CHAPTER I.

〔U0079 para〕
探究的重要性──我如何一步步得出結論──早期希臘女詩人眾多，消除了任何 *À PRIORI*（先驗的）困難──繆思女神與雅典娜為文學之首──闖入者是男人，而非女人。
> IMPORTANCE OF THE ENQUIRY—THE STEPS WHEREBY I WAS LED TO MY CONCLUSIONS—THE MULTITUDE OF EARLY GREEK POETESSES REMOVES ANY *À PRIORI* DIFFICULTY—THE MUSES AND MINERVA AS HEADS OF LITERATURE—MAN, RATHER THAN WOMAN, THE INTERLOPER.

〔U0080 para〕
《奧德賽》是男人還是女人寫的？它是否完全源於西西里？這兩個問題若除了確定作者的性別與居住地之外，不牽涉更大的問題，那麼也許只要提出答案，再請讀者去讀作品本身就夠了。然而，它們顯然對整個荷馬問題的爭論有重大影響；因為如果我們發現，女性的手筆在《奧德賽》中無所不在，又發現大量地方細節，如此專一、如此忠實地取材自西西里的單一城鎮，足以讓人相信作者必定曾在那裡生活和寫作，那麼這部詩出自一人之手的推定，似乎就無可抗拒了。因為在同一個地方，很難有一位以上的女性，能寫出我們在《奧德賽》全篇所見的那種詩──而且是那樣通體一致的詩。
> If the questions whether the *Odyssey* was written by a man or a woman, and whether or no it is of exclusively Sicilian origin, were pregnant with no larger issues than the determination of the sex and abode of the writer, it might be enough merely to suggest the answers and refer the reader to the work itself. Obviously, however, they have an important bearing on the whole Homeric controversy; for if we find a woman's hand omnipresent throughout the *Odyssey*, and if we also find so large a number of local details, taken so exclusively and so faithfully from a single Sicilian town as to warrant the belief that the writer must have lived and written there, the presumption seems irresistible that the poem was written by a single person. For there can hardly have been more than one woman in the same place able to write such—and such homogeneous—poetry as we find throughout the *Odyssey*.

〔U0081 para〕
許多問題將因此而簡化。其中之一是，我們可以把這部詩的年代限定在一個人的有生之年；而如果我們發現──我相信我們會發現──這個人很可能大約活躍於西元前 1050 年至 1000 年之間；此外，如果我們能證明──我們肯定能證明──她手邊有一部與我們今天所見大致相同的《伊里亞德》，並且無論有意無意，從最受懷疑的部分引用起來，與從公認出自荷馬的部分一樣隨意，那麼，對於解決《伊里亞德》是否同樣出自一人或多人之手的問題，我們就已大有貢獻。
> Many questions will become thus simplified. Among others we can limit the date of the poem to the lifetime of a single person, and if we find, as I believe we shall, that this person in all probability flourished, roughly between 1050 and 1000 B.C., if, moreover, we can show, as we assuredly can, that she had the *Iliad* before her much as we have it now, quoting, consciously or unconsciously, as freely from the most suspected parts as from those that are admittedly Homer's, we shall have done much towards settling the question whether the *Iliad* also is by one hand or by many.

〔U0082 para〕
倒不是說這個問題還需要費多少力氣去解決。認為《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》各由多人執筆，再在不同世紀由不同編者拼湊而成的理論，實在很難以敬意相待。它並非建立在任何其他以這種方式構成之詩篇的確鑿實例上；文學史上沒有任何一部詩，已知其成書過程像人們要我們硬套在《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》身上的那樣。這個理論的根基，是一種關於書寫何時成為可能的假設，而這個假設早已被證明站不住腳；它不僅沒有任何外部證據支持，還公然違背了我們僅有的那一點外部證據。它的基礎已經被人從底下抽掉，支撐它的論證卻從未能讓兩位學者得出相同的結論；而這類論證的性質，能把任何人引向任何結論，無論多麼荒謬，只要他事先已打定主意，要認為自己已經證明了那個結論。1892 年 1 月 2 日《旁觀者》上一位我不知其名的作者，在一篇文章結尾說：
> Not that this question ought to want much settling. The theory that the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* were written each of them by various hands, and pieced together in various centuries by various editors, is not one which it is easy to treat respectfully. It does not rest on the well established case of any other poem so constructed; literature furnishes us with no poem whose genesis is known to have been such as that which we are asked to foist upon the *Iliad* and *Odyssey*. The theory is founded on a supposition as to the date when writing became possible, which has long since been shown to be untenable; not only does it rest on no external evidence, but it flies in the face of what little external evidence we have. Based on a base that has been cut from under it, it has been sustained by arguments which have never succeeded in leading two scholars to the same conclusions, and which are of that character which will lead any one to any conclusion however preposterous, which he may have made up his mind to consider himself as having established. A writer in the *Spectator* of Jan. 2, 1892, whose name I do not know, concluded an article by saying,

〔U0083 quote〕
說世上最美好的詩是由眾多詩人各自貢獻拼湊而成，這違反我們所有的文學本能。
> That the finest poem of the world was created out of the contributions of a multitude of poets revolts all our literary instincts.

〔U0084 para〕
當然違反；但沃爾夫派的異端，經過或多或少的修正，至今在歐陸和英格蘭仍如此普遍地為人接受，要根除它並不容易。
> Of course it does, but the Wolfian heresy, more or less modified, is still so generally accepted both on the continent and in England that it will not be easy to exterminate it.

〔U0085 para〕
容易也好，不容易也罷，這件事都值得一試，因為沃爾夫的理論所孕育的禍害，遠比一眼看得出來的還要多。若不是沃爾夫實際上否定了荷馬的存在，攪亂了人心，誰會想到去攻擊莎士比亞的存在呢──因為莎士比亞若沒有寫他的劇本，他就不再是莎士比亞了？若不是人們發現，攪亂作者歸屬的問題是博取批評家名聲的捷徑，誰會去把歐洲半數畫廊裡的畫一幅接一幅地重新改定作者，往往肆意妄為，有時甚至無視最清楚的證據？而且這種事似乎沒完沒了，因為每一代人似乎都一心要在魯莽上勝過前一代。
> Easy or no this is a task well worth attempting, for Wolf's theory has been pregnant of harm in more ways than are immediately apparent. Who would have thought of attacking Shakspeare's existence—for if Shakspeare did not write his plays he is no longer Shakspeare—unless men's minds had been unsettled by Wolf's virtual denial of Homer's? Who would have reascribed picture after picture in half the galleries of Europe, often wantonly, and sometimes in defiance of the clearest evidence, if the unsettling of questions concerning authorship had not been found to be an easy road to reputation as a critic? Nor does there appear to be any end to it, for each succeeding generation seems bent on trying to surpass the recklessness of its predecessor.

〔U0086 para〕
不僅如此，以下各頁還將給某些人上另一種課──是什麼課，我留給讀者去猜；這些人我不點名，但讀者或許認得其中幾位，因為這種人多得很。說實在的，我有時想，這一課下得尖銳，或許比確立我立意要證明的那幾點，或比驅散德國教授們從自己內心意識中演化出來的那些荷馬式荒誕夢魘，都更有用處。
> And more than this, the following pages will read a lesson of another kind, which I will leave the reader to guess at, to men whom I will not name, but some of whom he may perhaps know, for there are many of them. Indeed I have sometimes thought that the sharpness of this lesson may be a more useful service than either the establishment of the points which I have set myself to prove, or the dispelling of the nightmares of Homeric extravagance which German professors have evolved out of their own inner consciousness.

〔U0087 para〕
有人或許會認為，一個正要力主《奧德賽》出自女性之手、源於西西里這兩項看似悖論之說的人，說這種話不太得體。一次這樣的衝擊已經夠糟了，兩次，而且每一次都影響如此深遠，就令人無法忍受。我感覺得到這一點，也為此感到沉重。當我回顧近 2500 年來關於《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》的爭論紀錄，想到它可以說完全與我相左；又想到學院利益錯綜複雜，更不用說投注在知名教科書與所謂教育上的商業利益──我怎能不對自己所承擔的任務之艱鉅、之僭越，甚至之全然無望，感到驚惶呢？
> Such language may be held to come ill from one who is setting himself to maintain two such seeming paradoxes as the feminine authorship, and Sicilian origin, of the *Odyssey*. One such shock would be bad enough, but two, and each so far-reaching, are intolerable. I feel this, and am oppressed by it. When I look back on the record of Iliadic and Odyssean controversy for nearly 2500 years, and reflect that it is, I may say, dead against me; when I reflect also upon the complexity of academic interests, not to mention the commercial interests vested in well-known school books and so-called education—how can I be other than dismayed at the magnitude, presumption, and indeed utter hopelessness, of the task I have undertaken?

〔U0088 para〕
我怎能指望荷馬學者容忍這些理論？它們顛覆了他們大多數人多年來一直堅持的一切。對他們或對我而言，這都是荷馬研究上（因為我的理論對《伊里亞德》問題的影響，幾乎和對《奧德賽》一樣大）生死攸關的事。如果我是對的，他們便把自己精明睿智的名聲投資在一支一文不值的股票上。舉例來說，如果《奧德賽》全部寫於特拉帕尼，全部出自一人之手，而那隻手是女人的手，那麼傑布教授著名的《荷馬導論》──這裡引用的是較短的書名──有很大一部分會變成什麼呢？要嘛我的著作是垃圾，那樣的話，不用粗魯的言語應該也不難證明；要嘛他們的著作有不少部分連印它的紙都不值。所以，他們若不對我稍加粗暴，那就是超乎常人了。
> How can I expect Homeric scholars to tolerate theories so subversive of all that most of them have been insisting on for so many years? It is a matter of Homeric (for my theory affects Iliadic questions nearly as much as it does the *Odyssey*) life and death for them or for myself. If I am right they have invested their reputation for sagacity in a worthless stock. What becomes, for example, of a great part of Professor Jebb's well-known *Introduction to Homer*—to quote his shorter title—if the *Odyssey* was written all of it at Trapani, all of it by one hand, and that hand a woman's? Either my own work is rubbish, in which case it should not be hard to prove it so without using discourteous language, or not a little of theirs is not worth the paper on which it is written. They will be more than human, therefore, if they do not handle me somewhat roughly.

〔U0089 para〕
至於《奧德賽》是女人寫的這一點，他們會告訴我，我連一個 *primâ facie*（表面成立）的論據都沒有建立起來。我當然沒有。建立它的是本特利，他說《伊里亞德》是為男人寫的，《奧德賽》是為女人寫的。[註1]文學史上沒有任何先例，是男人為女人而非為男人寫出一部偉大傑作的。如果一部匿名作品讓一位如此高明的批評家覺得它是為女人寫的，那麼「它很可能是女人寫的」這種想法，就有了表面成立的根據。不過，我否認《奧德賽》是為女人寫的；它是為任何願意聽的人寫的。本特利的意思是，《奧德賽》看事情是從女人而非男人的觀點出發；而他說出這個顯而易見的真理，我再說一次，就一勞永逸地建立了一個強有力的表面成立論據，足以讓人認為它是女人寫的。
> As for the *Odyssey* having been written by a woman, they will tell me that I have not even established a *primâ facie* case for my opinion. Of course I have not. It was Bentley who did this, when he said that the *Iliad* was written for men, and the *Odyssey* for women.[註1] The history of literature furnishes us with no case in which a man has written a great masterpiece for women rather than men. If an anonymous book strikes so able a critic as having been written for women, a *primâ facie* case is established for thinking that it was probably written by a woman. I deny, however, that the *Odyssey* was written for women; it was written for any one who would listen to it. What Bentley meant was that in the *Odyssey* things were looked at from a woman's point of view rather than a man's, and in uttering this obvious truth, I repeat, he established once for all a strong *primâ facie* case for thinking that it was written by a woman.

〔U0090 para〕
我的論證哪怕只有一行的九分之一能讓對手吹毛求疵，他們就會對其餘那八分──他們再怎麼粗暴地曲解也不敢下手的八分──不加理會，也隻字不提。他們會宣稱，《奧德賽》寫成時特拉帕尼沒有說希臘語的人，這對我的理論是致命的。他們先從自己對修昔底德（更別說維吉爾）所言一無所知的深淵裡撈出這個論斷──若把這兩位作家擱在一旁，那就是從他們對西元前十一世紀特拉帕尼有什麼、沒有什麼更深一層的無知裡撈出來的──然後便拒絕正視《奧德賽》本身提供的內證。他們會無視一個事實：修昔底德告訴我們，「來自特洛伊的那些福基斯人」曾定居在埃律克斯山，而我將會證明（見第十二章），這只能指福凱亞人；他們卻會反問我：修昔底德明明說定居在那裡的是福基斯人，我怎能把福凱亞人安置在埃律克斯山上？他們還會無視另一個事實：即使修昔底德只說「福基斯人」，而沒有加上「來自特洛伊的那些」或「特洛伊一支的」來限定，他仍然把說希臘語的人安置在離特拉帕尼不到五英里的地方。
> If my opponents can fasten a cavil on to the ninth part of a line of my argument, they will take no heed of, and make no reference to, the eight parts on which they dared not fasten a misrepresentation however gross. They will declare it fatal to my theory that there were no Greek-speaking people at Trapani when the *Odyssey* was written. Having fished up this assertion from the depths of their ignorance of what Thucydides, let alone Virgil, has told us,—or if they set these writers on one side, out of their still profounder ignorance of what there was or was not at Trapani in the eleventh century before Christ—they will refuse to look at the internal evidence furnished by the *Odyssey* itself. They will ignore the fact that Thucydides tells us that "Phocians of those from Troy," which as I will show (see Chapter XII.) can only mean Phocæans, settled at Mount Eryx, and ask me how I can place Phocæans on Mount Eryx when Thucydides says it was Phocians who settled there? They will ignore the fact that even though Thucydides had said "Phocians" without qualifying his words by adding "of those from Troy," or "of the Trojan branch," he still places Greek-speaking people within five miles of Trapani.

〔U0091 para〕
至於伊薩卡與斯刻里亞兩地都與特拉帕尼相符之處，他們會提醒我：弗魯愛林上尉曾找出蒙茅斯與馬其頓的相似之處，貝爾納迪諾．凱米也曾找出耶路撒冷與瓦拉洛塞西亞的相似之處；他們會說，如果單憑地形相似就能算數，那麼海峽群島比特拉帕尼外海的群島更像《奧德賽》所描述的愛奧尼亞群島，而巴拉克拉瓦呈現的整套斯刻里亞地形組合，又比特拉帕尼可信得多，究竟該選哪個地點，毫無疑問。我沒有去查巴拉克拉瓦的地圖，看看是否真是如此，也沒有去查別人向我推薦的其他同樣大有可為的地點；我只是舉例說明過去六年間我的理論一再遭受的批評，而我毫不懷疑，將來這些批評還會一再落到它頭上。
> As for the points of correspondence between both Ithaca and Scheria, and Trapani, they will remind me that Captain Fluelen found resemblances between Monmouth and Macedon, as also Bernardino Caimi did between Jerusalem and Varallo-Sesia; they will say that if mere topographical resemblances are to be considered, the Channel Islands are far more like the Ionian group as described in the *Odyssey* than those off Trapani are, while Balaclava presents us with the whole Scherian combination so far more plausibly than Trapani as to leave no doubt which site should be preferred. I have not looked at the map of Balaclava to see whether this is so or no, nor yet at other equally promising sites which have been offered me, but am limiting myself to giving examples of criticisms which have been repeatedly passed upon my theory during the last six years, and which I do not doubt will be repeatedly passed upon it in the future.

〔U0092 para〕
另一方面，我可以這樣安慰自己：不論我多麼該被石頭砸，也沒有人能問心無愧地拿石頭砸我。那些認為──如今大多數人都這麼認為──《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》屬於相隔好幾代的不同時代的人，想必總被這樣一個念頭糾纏：雖然兩千多年前就有許多人極力主張兩者作者不同，但從那時直到上世紀末，沒有一位荷馬研究者能被說服，承認我們如今視為不證自明的事。傑布教授談到本特利時[註2]說：
> On the other hand I may comfort myself by reflecting that however much I may deserve stoning there is no one who can stone me with a clear conscience. Those who hold, as most people now do, that the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* belong to ages separated from one another by some generations, must be haunted by the reflection that though the diversity of authorship was prominently insisted on by many people more than two thousand years ago, not a single Homeric student from those days to the end of the last century could be brought to acknowledge what we now deem self-evident. Professor Jebb, writing of Bentley,[註2] says

〔U0093 quote〕
他並沒有感覺到如今已普遍承認的一點：《奧德賽》帶有比《伊里亞德》更晚的時代印記。
> He had not felt what is now so generally admitted, that the *Odyssey* bears the marks of a later time than the *Iliad*.

〔U0094 para〕
像本特利這樣偉大的人物，怎麼會看不出如此明顯的事？誠如格萊斯頓先生所說，荷馬固然古老，對他系統而全面的研究卻還年輕。[註3]
> How came so great a man as Bentley not to see what is so obvious? Truly, as has been said by Mr. Gladstone, if Homer is old, the systematic and comprehensive study of him is still young.[註3]

〔U0095 para〕
我不打算爭論《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》是否出自同一人，因為如果我能說服讀者，《奧德賽》是一位女性在西西里寫成的，那麼它不是荷馬寫的也就不言而喻了，因為《伊里亞德》作者的性別是毋庸置疑的。迫使我們把《奧德賽》歸於女性的那些準則，同樣不容許我們對《伊里亞德》得出「出自男人之手」以外的任何結論。因此，我將直接進入《奧德賽》究竟是男人還是女人所寫的問題。
> I shall not argue the question whether the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* are by the same person, inasmuch as if I convince the reader that the *Odyssey* was written by a woman and in Sicily, it will go without saying that it was not written by Homer; for there can be no doubt about the sex of the writer of the *Iliad*. The same canons which will compel us to ascribe the *Odyssey* to a woman forbid any other conclusion than that the *Iliad* was written by a man. I shall therefore proceed at once to the question whether the *Odyssey* was written by a man or by a woman.

〔U0096 para〕
俗話說，沒有人能替別人做得比替自己做得更好；所以，要說服讀者，也許最好的辦法是回頭追述我是循著哪些步驟，得出我請讀者接受的那些結論。
> It is an old saying that no man can do better for another than he can for himself, I may perhaps therefore best succeed in convincing the reader if I retrace the steps by which I arrived at the conclusions I ask him to adopt.

〔U0097 para〕
我之所以著手研究《奧德賽》，是因為我替一部世俗神劇《奧德修斯》寫了歌詞和大部分音樂──我和朋友 H．費斯廷．瓊斯先生已經合作這部作品一段時間了。到了這一步，我忽然想到，終究還是應該看看《奧德賽》本身怎麼說；又找不到可讀的散文譯本，只好去讀原文，而我已經大約三十五年連想都沒有想過原文了。
> I was led to take up the *Odyssey* by having written the libretto and much of the music for a secular oratorio, *Ulysses*, on which my friend Mr. H. Festing Jones and I had been for some time engaged. Having reached this point it occurred to me that I had better after all see what the *Odyssey* said, and finding no readable prose translation, was driven to the original, to which I had not given so much as a thought for some five and thirty years.

〔U0098 para〕
希臘文很淺易，我讀起來沒什麼困難，而且我是帶著一雙新鮮的眼睛來讀這部詩，這是一大優勢。此外，我從頭到尾把它讀完了，此後我又這樣讀過許多遍。
> The Greek being easy, I had little difficulty in understanding what I read, and I had the great advantage of coming to the poem with fresh eyes. Also, I read it all through from end to end, as I have since many times done.

〔U0099 para〕
然而，儘管它驚人的趣味與美立刻令我著迷，我卻始終感覺到有什麼不對勁，有什麼東西在躲著我，有一個我解不開的謎。我愈是思索那些如此明亮、如此透澈的文字，就愈覺得它們背後有一片黑暗，我必須穿透它，才能看見作者的心──而這正是我想要的；因為藝術之所以有趣，只在於它揭示了一位藝術家。
> Fascinated, however, as I at once was by its amazing interest and beauty, I had an ever-present sense of a something wrong, of a something that was eluding me, and of a riddle which I could not read. The more I reflected upon the words, so luminous and so transparent, the more I felt a darkness behind them, that I must pierce before I could see the heart of the writer—and this was what I wanted; for art is only interesting in so far as it reveals an artist.

〔U0100 para〕
為了更理解這部詩，我開始把它譯成平實的散文，對──比方說──托登罕宮路懷著善意的偏好，就像布徹與朗兩位先生對沃德街所懷的偏好一樣。不過我承認，沃德街英語自有它可取之處。比方說，《古舟子詠》若題作《老水手》，就會失色不少；但大體而言，我認為像《奧德賽》這樣絲毫不沾矯揉造作的故事，以不加矯飾的方式講述，才最能暢行無阻。
> In the hope of getting to understand the poem better I set about translating it into plain prose, with the same benevolent leaning, say, towards Tottenham Court Road, that Messrs. Butcher and Lang have shewn towards Wardour Street. I admit, however, that Wardour Street English has something to say for itself. The "Ancient Mariner," for example, would have lost a good deal if it had been called "The Old Sailor," but on the whole I take it that a tale so absolutely without any taint of affectation as the *Odyssey* will speed best being unaffectedly told.

〔U0101 para〕
讀到費埃克斯人的段落時，我確信至少在這裡，作者是取材於現實生活，瑙西卡、阿瑞忒王后與阿爾基諾俄斯是真實的人物，只是多少經過喬裝改扮；翻開繆爾上校的著作[註4]，我發現他也持同樣看法。然而，我仍不斷對書中男人說話行事的方式大感驚愕──尤其是卷八所描寫的、為奧德修斯舉行的那場競技。繆爾上校說（第 407 頁）：「這個群體所分配到的那一點點常識，主要都被女人包辦了。」確實如此，但我從沒想到要問問自己：男人通常會寫出女人比男人更明理的精彩作品嗎？我仍受作者是男人這個想法支配，便猜想他也許是某位吟遊詩人，或許是個盲人，住在僕役之間，就像兩百年前我們這裡大戶人家裡的家庭牧師那樣。這樣一位吟遊詩人，即使不瞎，也只能遠遠地看見那些大人物，不會與他們往來密切到知道他們私下會怎樣說話行事。我甚至從沒想到，瞎的可能是那些註釋家，而他們或許因此才認為詩人必定也是瞎的。
> When I came to the Phæacian episode I felt sure that here at any rate the writer was drawing from life, and that Nausicaa, Queen Arete, and Alcinous were real people more or less travestied, and on turning to Colonel Mure's work[註4] I saw that he was of the same opinion. Nevertheless I found myself continually aghast at the manner in which men were made to speak and act—especially, for example, during the games in honour of *Ulysses* described in Book viii. Colonel Mure says (p. 407) that "the women engross the chief part of the small stock of common sense allotted to the community." So they do, but it never occurred to me to ask myself whether men commonly write brilliant books in which the women are made more sensible than the men. Still dominated by the idea that the writer was a man, I conjectured that he might be some bard, perhaps blind, who lived among the servants much as the chaplain in a great house a couple of hundred years ago among ourselves. Such a bard, even though not blind, would only see great people from a distance, and would not mix with them intimately enough to know how they would speak and act among themselves. It never even crossed my mind that it might have been the commentators who were blind, and that they might have thus come to think that the poet must have been blind too.

〔U0102 para〕
作者或許多半住在管家房裡，而不是與宅中的大人物在一起──這個想法（我說來慚愧）讓我安心了一陣子；但不久我就發覺，書中的男人雖然常說些、做些沒有男人會說會做的事，女人卻只要作者有意，總是十足的淑女。一個僕役廳裡的吟遊詩人，怎麼會在男人身上屢屢錯得無可救藥，卻在他筆下每一個女人身上都對得如此精妙？但我還是沒有領悟。直到讀到喀耳刻，我才猛然想到，我讀的不是一位老人的作品，而是一位年輕女子的作品──而且這位女子對男人能做什麼、不能做什麼所知不多，正如我發現她對在波呂斐摩斯洞穴裡擠母羊奶這件事所知不多一樣。
> The view that the writer might have lived more in the steward's room than with the great people of the house served (I say it with shame) to quiet me for a time, but by and by it struck me that though the men often both said and did things that no man would say or do, the women were always ladies when the writer chose to make them so. How could it be that a servant's hall bard should so often go hopelessly wrong with his men, and yet be so exquisitively right with every single one of his women? But still I did not catch it. It was not till I got to Circe that it flashed upon me that I was reading the work, not of an old man, but of a young woman—and of one who knew not much more about what men can and cannot do than I had found her know about the milking of ewes in the cave of Polyphemus.

〔U0103 para〕
我愈想愈驚訝於自己的愚鈍，因為我記得自己還是個在學的男孩時，常說《奧德賽》是《伊里亞德》的妻子，又說它是一位牧師寫的。但不管怎樣，作者是女人──而且是年輕女人──這個想法一出現在我面前，我就覺得這正是困擾我許久的那個謎的謎底。我試著擺脫它，它卻不肯走；只要我守著它，一切就都連貫起來，各安其位；一把它擱在一邊，一切又都不對了。我並沒有去尋找我的結論；我甚至不認得它的模樣，無從去找它；是它主動上前與我搭話，自我介紹說它就是我的結論，並發誓永遠不離開我；於是，我被它的儀表打動，讓它留在我身邊試用一兩個星期，在這段期間，它的一言一行都恰如其分，令我著迷，然後我便命它躋身我最堅定信奉的那些信念之列。不過不用說，又過了很久，我才看出這部詩全部是在特拉帕尼寫成的，而作者以瑙西卡之名把自己寫進了作品裡。
> The more I think of it the more I wonder at my own stupidity, for I remember that when I was a boy at school I used to say the *Odyssey* was the *Iliad's* wife, and that it was written by a clergyman. But however this may be, as soon as the idea that the writer was a woman—and a young one—presented itself to me, I felt that here was the reading of the riddle that had so long baffled me. I tried to divest myself of it, but it would not go; as long as I kept to it, everything cohered and was in its right place, and when I set it aside all was wrong again; I did not seek my conclusion; I did not even know it by sight so as to look for it; it accosted me, introduced itself as my conclusion, and vowed that it would never leave me; whereon, being struck with its appearance, I let it stay with me on probation for a week or two during which I was charmed with the propriety of all it said or did, and then bade it take rank with the convictions to which I was most firmly wedded; but I need hardly say that it was a long time before I came to see that the poem was all of it written at Trapani, and that the writer had introduced herself into her work under the name of Nausicaa.

〔U0104 para〕
這些問題我稍後再談，但我要指出：一旦我們拒絕把《奧德賽》歸於《伊里亞德》的作者（只有他才該稱作荷馬），它就成了一部匿名作品；而批評家考察一部匿名作品時，首先要著手確定的就是作者的性別。即使女人假扮男人，這件事也很少困難──事實上，每當有匿名作品出版，幾乎每次都能成功判定──而當一個人寫作時那樣坦率、那樣自然流露，像《奧德賽》那樣散發令人無法抗拒的魅力，這件事不僅不難，而且極其容易；只有在批評家──就像我們在這件事上全都如此──被一種根深蒂固的成見所支配，同時「作者是女人」這個假設又帶有某種強烈的 *à priori*（先驗的）不可能性時，困難才會出現。
> I will deal with these points later, but would point out that the moment we refuse to attribute the *Odyssey* to the writer of the *Iliad* (whom we should alone call Homer) it becomes an anonymous work; and the first thing that a critic will set himself to do when he considers an anonymous work is to determine the sex of the writer. This, even when women are posing as men, is seldom difficult—indeed it is done almost invariably with success as often as an anonymous work is published—and when any one writes with the frankness and spontaneity which are such an irresistible charm in the *Odyssey*, it is not only not difficult but exceedingly easy; difficulty will only arise, if the critic is, as we have all been in this case, dominated by a deeply-rooted preconceived opinion, and if also there is some strong *à priori* improbability in the supposition that the writer was a woman.

〔U0105 para〕
有人會強調，任何時代的任何女人寫出《奧德賽》這樣的傑作，都是極不可能的事。可是，任何男人寫出這樣的作品，也同樣極不可能。自《奧德賽》寫成以來的這許多百年間，沒有一個男人寫得出另一部能與它相比的作品。斯特拉福一個羊毛商的兒子寫出《哈姆雷特》，或者貝德福郡一個補鍋匠寫出《天路歷程》這樣的傑作，都是極不可能的事。非凡的作品意味著非凡的作者，但非凡的女人與非凡的男人一樣存在；而且，雖然《伊里亞德》裡有許多內容，很難設想有哪個女人──不論多麼非凡──可能寫得出來，《奧德賽》裡卻沒有一行是女人寫不出來的，而且其中有許多美，是男人幾乎一定會忽略的。此外，《奧德賽》中有許多錯誤，是年輕女人很容易犯、男人卻很難犯的──例如，在卷二結尾讓風在浪頭上呼嘯；以為一隻小羊可以靠每天吸兩口已經擠過奶的母羊的奶活下去（卷九 244–245 行，以及 308–309 行）；以為船的兩頭都有舵（卷九 483、540 行）；以為可以從一棵生長中的樹砍下乾燥、風乾良好的木材（卷五 240 行）；讓一隻鷹還在飛行中就撕扯獵物──這是沒有一隻鷹做得到的（卷十五 527 行）。
> It may be urged that it is extremely improbable that any woman in any age should write such a masterpiece as the *Odyssey*. But so it also is that any man should do so. In all the many hundreds of years since the *Odyssey* was written, no man has been able to write another that will compare with it. It was extremely improbable that the son of a Stratford wool-stapler should write *Hamlet*, or that a Bedfordshire tinker should produce such a masterpiece as *Pilgrim's Progress*. Phenomenal works imply a phenomenal workman, but there are phenomenal women as well as phenomenal men, and though there is much in the *Iliad* which no woman, however phenomenal, can be supposed at all likely to have written, there is not a line in the *Odyssey* which a woman might not perfectly well write, and there is much beauty which a man would be almost certain to neglect. Moreover there are many mistakes in the *Odyssey* which a young woman might easily make, but which a man could hardly fall into—for example, making the wind whistle over waves at the end of Book ii., thinking that a lamb could live on two pulls a day at a ewe that was already milked (ix. 244, 245, and 308, 309), believing a ship to have a rudder at both ends (ix. 483, 540), thinking that dry and well-seasoned timber can be cut from a growing tree (v. 240), making a hawk while still on the wing tear its prey—a thing that no hawk can do (xv. 527).

〔U0106 para〕
我注意到布徹與朗兩位先生略去了卷九 483 行，這一行把舵放在船頭；但它確實見於文本，而且是抄寫者最不會想插進去的那種敘述。儘管如此，要不是我也發現作者在卷五 255 行解釋說，奧德修斯給木筏裝上舵，「好讓他能夠掌控方向」，我倒也狠得下心認定是文本出了錯。對舵已有明確概念的人，會認為船靠舵掌控方向這件事不言而喻。不僅如此，她不但解釋奧德修斯需要一個舵來掌控方向，後來（卷五 270 行）還告訴我們，他做好舵之後真的用了它，而且用得 τεχνηέντως，也就是很有技巧。
> I see that Messrs. Butcher and Lang omit ix. 483 in which the rudder is placed in the bows of a ship, but it is found in the text, and is the last kind of statement a copyist would be inclined to intercalate. Yet I could have found it in my heart to conceive the text in fault, had I not also found the writer explaining in Book v. 255 that Ulysses gave his raft a rudder "in order that he might be able to steer it." People whose ideas about rudders have become well defined will let the fact that a ship is steered by means of its rudder go without saying. Furthermore, not only does she explain that Ulysses would want a rudder to steer with, but later on (line 270) she tells us that he actually did use the rudder when he had made it, and, moreover, that he used it τεχνηέντως, or skilfully.

〔U0107 para〕
年輕女子都知道馬是走在車子前頭的，聽說舵引導船隻，往往就會──我不只一次發現她們這樣──以為舵是走在船前面的。《奧德賽》的作者大概一時忘了舵該裝在哪一頭。她昨天把這件事從頭想過了，今天不打算再從頭想一遍，於是把舵裝在兩頭，打算等確定哪一頭是錯的，再把那一頭的拿掉；後來她忘了這回事，或者覺得這麼小的細節不值得再費心。
> Young women know that a horse goes before a cart, and being told that the rudder guides the ship, are apt—and I have more than once found them do so—to believe that it goes in front of the ship. Probably the writer of the *Odyssey* forgot for the moment at which end the rudder should be. She thought it all over yesterday, and was not going to think it all over again to-day, so she put the rudder at both ends, intending to remove it from the one that should prove to be the wrong one; later on she forgot, or did not think it worth while to trouble about so small a detail.

〔U0108 para〕
卡呂普索的斧頭也是如此（卷五 234–236 行）。慣於使用斧頭的人，絕不會把它描寫得那麼詳盡，告訴我們它「合奧德修斯的手」，還裝有斧柄。我聽說，有一位著名的女作家之所以被識破是女人，是因為她說了兩英尺的 *ruler*（尺子），而不是兩英尺的 *rule*（量尺）；但描寫過分細緻，比用錯名稱更深刻、更有力地證明了對事物的不熟悉。
> So with Calypso's axe (v. 234-36). No one who was used to handling an axe would describe it so fully and tell us that it "suited Ulysses' hands," and was furnished with a handle. I have heard say that a celebrated female authoress was discovered to be a woman by her having spoken of a two-foot *ruler* instead of a two-foot *rule*, but over minuteness of description is deeper and stronger evidence of unfamiliarity than mistaken nomenclature is.

〔U0109 para〕
上面指出的這類錯誤和自我暴露，非但無損於《奧德賽》的魅力，反而更添魅力。就算《奧德賽》在力量、雄健與詩意意象的豐富上不及《伊里亞德》，我也不認為它在吸引讀者的能力上有所不及。說實在的，如果兩者必須犧牲其一，我幾乎可以確定，我會捨《伊里亞德》而留《奧德賽》──正如如果白朗峰與羅莎峰必須犧牲其一，我會犧牲白朗峰，儘管我知道就許多方面而言，它是兩座山中較雄偉的一座。[註5]
> Such mistakes and self-betrayals as those above pointed out enhance rather than impair the charm of the *Odyssey*. Granted that the *Odyssey* is inferior to the *Iliad* in strength, robustness, and wealth of poetic imagery, I cannot think that it is inferior in its power of fascinating the reader. Indeed, if I had to sacrifice one or the other, I can hardly doubt that I should let the *Iliad* go rather than the *Odyssey*—just as if I had to sacrifice either Mont Blanc or Monte Rosa, I should sacrifice Mont Blanc, though I know it to be in many respects the grander mountain of the two.[註5]

〔U0110 para〕
不過，不言而喻，女人作品中許多迷人之處，放在男人作品中就會顯得可笑，而《奧德賽》正是這一點的絕佳例證。如果它是女人寫的，它就如本書的卷首插圖一樣可愛，並且雖然不那麼雄健，卻無疑比《伊里亞德》更奇妙；反之，如果它是男人寫的，那麼它給我們的那種感受──一半是巴約掛毯，一半是波提切利那幅從海中升起的〈維納斯〉，或是〈春〉──就會讓位給驚訝：怎麼會有男人寫得出這樣的作品。對女人是得體的方式，對男人就是不得體的方式，*vice versa*（反之亦然）。舉例來說，珍．奧斯汀筆下的年輕男子很少有趣；但只有那些看不見珍．奧斯汀作品中精妙真實與細膩的人，才會想抱怨她對年輕男子的理解不如對年輕女子。
> It should go, however, without saying that much which is charming in a woman's work would be ridiculous in a man's, and this is eminently exemplified in the *Odyssey*. If a woman wrote it, it is as lovely as the frontispiece of this volume, and becomes, if less vigorous, yet assuredly more wonderful than the *Iliad*; if, on the other hand, it is by a man, the half Bayeux tapestry, half Botticelli's Venus rising from the sea, or Primavera, feeling with which it impresses us gives place to astonishment how any man could have written it. What is a right manner for a woman is a wrong one for a man, and *vice versa*. Jane Austen's young men, for example, are seldom very interesting, but it is only those who are blind to the exquisite truth and delicacy of Jane Austen's work who will feel any wish to complain of her for not understanding young men as well as she did young women.

〔U0111 para〕
《泰晤士報》一篇社論（1897 年 2 月 4 日）的作者說：
> The writer of a *Times* leading article (Feb. 4th, 1897) says:

〔U0112 para〕
性別差異是人類之間最深刻、影響最深遠的差異……女人在智力上或許與男人相等，或許不相等；……但女人整體而言會以女人的方式行事，而那不是、也永遠不可能是男人的方式。
> The sex difference is the profoundest and most far-reaching that exists among human beings.... Women may or may not be the equals of men in intelligence;... but women in the mass will act after the manner of women, which is not and never can be the manner of men.

〔U0113 para〕
她們怎樣行事，就會怎樣寫作。然而，當她們的作品在同類中屬於佳作時，這並不會使它稍減魅力；恰恰相反，反而更添魅力。
> And as they will act, so will they write. This, however, does not make their work any the less charming when it is good of its kind; on the contrary, it makes it more so.

〔U0114 para〕
因此，撇開「任何女人能否寫出《奧德賽》這樣奇妙的詩」這個難題不談，要認為這樣一位女人可能曾經存在──比方說在西元前 1000 年──有沒有任何 *à priori*（先驗的）障礙？我知道沒有。希臘文學要到大約西元前 600 年，才開始在我們眼前露出曙光。在此之前，除了《伊里亞德》《奧德賽》和那位迷人的作家赫西俄德之外，我們幾乎一無所有。然而，一旦來到最早的歷史文獻，我們就發現著名的女詩人比比皆是。
> Dismissing, therefore, the difficulty of supposing that any woman could write so wonderful a poem as the *Odyssey*, is there any *à priori* obstacle to our thinking that such a woman may have existed, say, B.C. 1000? I know of none. Greek literature does not begin to dawn upon us till about 600 B.C. Earlier than this date we have hardly anything except the *Iliad*, *Odyssey*, and that charming writer Hesiod. When, however, we come to the earliest historic literature we find that famous poetesses abounded.

〔U0115 para〕
翻閱史密斯《古典人物辭典》中「莎芙」條目的人，會看到其中提到戈爾戈與安德洛墨達是她的對手。她的同伴有米利都的阿娜克托莉亞、科洛封的貢古勒、薩拉米斯的歐妮卡、古琳娜、阿提斯與姆娜西狄卡。該條作者說：「以作品享有最高聲譽的，是潘菲利亞人達摩菲利亞，以及特洛斯島的艾琳娜。」後面這位女詩人寫過一首關於紡紗桿的長詩，被認為足以與荷馬本人相提並論──所指的大概是《奧德賽》。
> Those who turn to the article "Sappho" in Smith's *Dictionary of Classical Biography* will find Gorgo and Andromeda mentioned as her rivals. Among her fellows were Anactoria of Miletus, Gongyle of Colophon, Eunica of Salamis, Gyrinna, Atthis, and Mnasidica, "Those," says the writer, "who attained the highest celebrity for their works were Damophylia the Pamphylian, and Erinna of Telos." This last-named poetess wrote a long poem upon the distaff, which was considered equal to Homer himself—the *Odyssey* being probably intended.

〔U0116 para〕
此外還有鮑基斯，她寫了〈艾琳娜墓誌〉。翻開繆勒論多利安人的著作，我發現其中提到萊斯博斯的愛情女詩人。他還告訴我們有科林娜，據說她曾在比賽中勝過品達；還有默爾托，她確實曾與品達比賽，但結果如何我們不得而知。此外還有阿卡狄亞人狄奧提瑪；翻閱貝爾克的《希臘抒情詩人集》（*Poetœ Lyrici Grœci*），我又找到其他一些女性的名字，她們的作品殘篇透過現存作家的引用流傳到我們手中。希伯來人當中則有米利暗、底波拉和哈拿，一般認為她們都比《奧德賽》早好幾個世紀。
> Again, there was Baucis, who wrote Erinna's Epitaph. Turning to Müller's work upon the Dorians, I find reference made to the amatory poetesses of Lesbos. He tells us also of Corinna, who is said to have competed successfully with Pindar, and Myrto, who certainly competed with him, but with what success we know not. Again, there was Diotima the Arcadian; and looking through Bergk's *Poetœ Lyrici Grœci* I find other names of women, fragments of whose works have readied us through quotation by extant writers. Among the Hebrews there were Miriam, Deborah, and Hannah, all of them believed to be centuries older than the *Odyssey*.

〔U0117 para〕
那麼，既然我們知道，在希臘文學已知最早的時代，女詩人在希臘、小亞細亞與愛琴海諸島的廣大地區如此眾多，就沒有理由拒絕承認西元前 1000 年西西里可能住著一位希臘女詩人；尤其是我們從修昔底德得知，我認為她所居住的那一帶西西里，早在荷馬時代結束前好幾個世紀，就已有來自小亞細亞西北角的人前來殖民。《奧德賽》所描繪的文明，與西元前 600 至 500 年間米蒂利尼或米洛斯可能有過的任何文明一樣先進；而在《伊里亞德》和《奧德賽》中，女性的地位都被描寫得與今日大致相同，並且遠遠高於我們最熟悉的雅典文明中的女性地位。設想伯里克利時代的雅典有一位偉大的希臘女詩人，那是違反常理的；但我幾乎可以說，在一個女性像《奧德賽》中所呈現的那樣自由的時代，假定那時沒有女詩人，才是違反常理。
> If, then, poetesses were as abundant as we know them to have been in the earliest known ages of Greek literature over a wide area in Greece, Asia Minor, and the islands of the Ægæan, there is no ground for refusing to admit the possibility that a Greek poetess lived in Sicily B.C. 1000, especially when we know from Thucydides that the particular part of Sicily where I suppose her to have lived was colonised from the North West corner of Asia Minor centuries before the close of the Homeric age. The civilisation depicted in the *Odyssey* is as advanced as any that is likely to have existed in Mitylene or Melos 600—500 B.C., while in both the *Iliad* and the *Odyssey* the status of women is represented as being much what it is at the present, and as incomparably higher than it was in the Athenian civilisation with which we are best acquainted. To imagine a great Greek poetess at Athens in the age of Pericles would be to violate probability, but I might almost say in an age when women were as free as they are represented to us in the *Odyssey* it is a violation of probability to suppose that there were no poetesses.

〔U0118 para〕
我們沒有理由認為男人比女人更早學會使用舌頭；那麼，當使用筆已經成為常事時，我們又為什麼要假定女人落在男人後面呢？如果一個女人能像海倫那樣用針繡出圖畫[註6]，就像征服者威廉的妻子在一個非常相似的文明中所做的那樣，那麼只要她有意，她也能用筆寫出故事。
> We have no reason to think that men found the use of their tongue sooner than women did; why then should we suppose that women lagged behind men when the use of the pen had become familiar? If a woman could work pictures with her needle as Helen did,[註6] and as the wife of William the Conqueror did in a very similiar civilisation, she could write stories with her pen if she had a mind to do so.

〔U0119 para〕
荷馬時代公認的文學之首是九位繆思女神──因為所涉及的總是她們，或是「繆思」，從來不是阿波羅或雅典娜──這一事實把女性作者的可能性推回到極其久遠的時代；那時，凡是作者就一定是詩人，因為散文寫作是相對晚近才發展出來的。《伊里亞德》和《奧德賽》都以向一位女性的祈求開篇；她既是文學之首，就必須假定她是一位女作者，儘管她的作品沒有一部流傳到我們手中。此外，在一個男人主要忙於打仗或打獵的時代，和平的技藝──其中包括各種文學才藝──更自然地會留給女人。如果真相大白，我們很可能會發現，在文學的領域裡，闖入者是男人而不是女人。瑙西卡比較可能是一種遺存，而不是闖入者；但最可能的是，她正處在時尚的最前端。
> The fact that the recognised heads of literature in the Homeric age were the nine Muses—for it is always these or "The Muse" that is involved, and never Apollo or Minerva—throws back the suggestion of female authorship to a very remote period, when, to be an author at all, was to be a poet, for prose writing is a comparatively late development. Both *Iliad* and *Odyssey* begin with an invocation addressed to a woman, who, as the head of literature, must be supposed to have been an authoress, though none of her works have come down to us. In an age, moreover, when men were chiefly occupied either with fighting or hunting, the arts of peace, and among them all kinds of literary accomplishment, would be more naturally left to women. If the truth were known, we might very likely find that it was man rather than woman who has been the interloper in the domain of literature. Nausicaa was more probably a survival than an interloper, but most probably of all she was in the height of the fashion.
