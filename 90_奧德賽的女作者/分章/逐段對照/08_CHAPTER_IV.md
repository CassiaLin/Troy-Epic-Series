〔U0700 heading〕
第四章
> CHAPTER IV.

〔U0701 para〕
為女性的榮譽與尊嚴而戒慎──對玷辱同性者嚴厲無情──喜愛瑣細的宗教儀節──喜愛說教──喜愛善意的小謊與小小的作戲──喜愛兩面討好──以及喜愛錢財。
> JEALOUSY FOR THE HONOUR AND DIGNITY OF WOMAN—SEVERITY AGAINST THOSE WHO HAVE DISGRACED THEIR SEX—LOVE OF SMALL RELIGIOUS OBSERVANCES—OF PREACHING—OF WHITE LIES AND SMALL PLAY-ACTING—OF HAVING THINGS BOTH WAYS—AND OF MONEY.

〔U0702 para〕
作者處理女性時，不但顯露出明顯更多的興趣與更豐富的知識，而且她清楚表明，她對自己性別的榮譽極為戒慎，因此對那些玷辱了這份榮譽的女人，也就嚴厲得毫不留情。女神愛做什麼就做什麼，不受凡人的律法評判；但凡間女子一旦失足，就非死不可。
> Not only does the writer shew a markedly greater both interest and knowledge when dealing with women, but she makes it plain that she is exceedingly jealous for the honour of her sex, and by consequence inexorable in her severity against those women who have disgraced it. Goddesses may do what they like, they are not to be judged by mortal codes; but a mortal woman who has fallen must die.

〔U0703 para〕
整部《奧德賽》裡，從來沒有哪個女人被取笑。女人可以被吊死，卻不可以被取笑。男人可以被取笑；其實，阿爾基諾俄斯幾乎每次被提到，都是為了讓他多少顯得可笑。我們不能說卷四和卷十五裡的墨涅拉俄斯是被刻意寫得可笑，但他確實顯得可笑，而且被當成一個遠不如他妻子有趣、也遠不如她重要的人物。事實上，奧德修斯、阿爾基諾俄斯、墨涅拉俄斯和涅斯托爾彼此如此相像，我毫不懷疑他們是以同一個人為原型畫出來的，正如伊薩卡和斯刻里亞是以同一個地方為原型。那個人是誰，我們永遠無從知曉；不過我要指出，除非女兒崇拜她的父親，否則父親對她來說，通常是個神祕而有權勢的存在，他的行事與她的行事截然不同。她怕他，就像孩子怕黑房間；而如果他的妻子有點喜歡取笑他，女兒也不會放過他，無論她多麼會哄他，又以某種方式愛著他。
> No woman throughout the *Odyssey* is ever laughed at. Women may be hanged but they must not be laughed at. Men may be laughed at, indeed Alcinous is hardly mentioned at all except to be made more or less ridiculous. One cannot say that Menelaus in Books iv. and xv. is being deliberately made ridiculous, but made ridiculous he certainly is, and he is treated as a person of far less interest and importance than his wife is. Indeed Ulysses, Alcinous, Menelaus, and Nestor are all so like one another that I do not doubt they were drawn from the same person, just as Ithaca and Scheria are from the same place. Who that person was we shall never know; nevertheless I would point out that unless a girl adores her father he is generally, to her, a mysterious powerful being whose ways are not as her ways. He is feared as a dark room is feared by children; and if his wife is at all given to laughing at him, his daughter will not spare him, however much she may cajole and in a way love him.

〔U0704 para〕
但是，如我說過的，男人雖然可以被取笑，女人卻從來都被認真對待。阿芙蘿黛蒂倒是在某一段裡顯得有點可笑，但她是女神，所以無妨；況且，嘲笑的矛頭主要是由阿瑞斯承受的，而阿芙蘿黛蒂立刻就由美惠三女神重新裝扮、好言撫慰了。我想不起有哪一個例子，是讓女人去做一件她做了就會有失身分的事──當然，奴隸除外，我說的是上層社會。
> But, as I have said, though men may be laughed at, the women are never taken other than quite seriously. Venus is indeed, made a little ridiculous in one passage, but she was a goddess, so it does not matter; besides, the brunt of the ridicule was borne by Mars, and Venus was instantly readorned and comforted by the Graces. I cannot remember a single instance of a woman's being made to do anything which she could not do without loss of dignity—I except, of course, slaves, and am speaking of the higher social classes.

〔U0705 para〕
常有人指出，《伊里亞德》裡眾神的使者總是伊里斯，而在《奧德賽》裡，這個角色同樣一律是赫耳墨斯。我傾向於把這歸因於作者不喜歡讓伊里斯這樣一位高貴的女士替任何人跑腿聽差。因為顯然，一般人仍然認為伊里斯一向是眾神的使者。這從卷十八開頭可以看出：詩中告訴我們，伊羅斯的本名是阿爾奈俄斯，但人家叫他伊羅斯（這不過就是 Iris 加上一個陽性詞尾），「因為只要有人差遣，他就去傳話」。作家們除非有自己的特殊理由，是不會公然違背流行說法的。
> It has often been observed that the Messenger of the Gods in the *Iliad* is always Iris, while in the *Odyssey* he is no less invariably Mercury. I incline to attribute this to the author's dislike of the idea that so noble a lady as Iris should be made to fetch and carry for anybody. For it is evident Iris was still generally held to have been the messenger of the gods. This appears from the beginning of Book xviii., where we are told that Irus's real name was Arnæus, but that he was called Irus (which is nothing but Iris with a masculine termination) "because he used to carry messages when any one would send him." Writers do not fly in the face of current versions unless for some special reasons of their own.

〔U0706 para〕
然而，女人若是行為不端，就絕不寬貸。相關的例子只有三個，其中一個幾乎不算數，因為那個有罪的女人──克呂泰涅斯特拉──所受的懲罰，並不是女作者親手施加的。不過，克呂泰涅斯特拉罪行的故事緊緊抓住了她，她一再回到這個話題，她對此深感恐懼，卻又同時想盡量替克呂泰涅斯特拉卸下罪責──這一切使我確信，她深切感受到克呂泰涅斯特拉的背叛給全體女性帶來的恥辱，「連好女人也不能倖免」。不然她為什麼要費這麼大的力氣告訴我們，克呂泰涅斯特拉天性本來善良（卷三 266 行），而且在阿伽門農託付保護她的那位歌人被害死之前，她一直無可指摘？[註1]等到她孤身一人──既沒有丈夫，也沒有監護人，又有埃癸斯托斯這樣陰險的卑鄙小人用不斷的奉承來引誘她──她便屈服了；此外就沒什麼好說的了，只能說這實在太可怕，她只好聽任命運處置。我看到格萊斯頓先生曾納悶，是什麼促使荷馬（他認為《伊里亞德》與《奧德賽》都是荷馬所作）告訴我們克呂泰涅斯特拉起初是個好女人[註2]；但是，儘管我十分敬重他對荷馬文學的偉大貢獻，我卻不認為他找到了正確的解釋。此外也不該忘記，這種替克呂泰涅斯特拉開脫罪責的寫法，屬於《奧德賽》中後來嫁接到原始構想上的部分──在這部分裡，如我稍後將要證明的，還有另一個女人的罪過，它之所以沒有被開脫，只是因為它在壓倒性的證據面前被徹底否認了──我指的是潘妮洛碧的罪過。
> If, however, a woman has misconducted herself she is to be shewn no mercy. There are only three cases in point, and one of these hardly counts inasmuch as the punishment of the guilty woman, Clytemnestra, was not meted out to her by the authoress herself. The hold, however, which the story of Clytemnestra's guilt has upon her, the manner in which she repeatedly recurs to it, her horror at it, but at the same time her desire to remove as much of the blame as possible from Clytemnestra's shoulders, convinces me that she acutely feels the disgrace which Clytemnestra's treachery has inflicted upon all women "even on the good ones." Why should she be at such pains to tell us that Clytemnestra was a person of good natural disposition (iii. 266), and was irreproachable until death had removed the bard under whose protection Agamemnon had placed her?[註1] When she was left alone—without either husband or guardian, and with an insidious wretch like Ægisthus beguiling her with his incessant flattery, she yielded, and there is no more to be said, except that it was very dreadful and she must be abandoned to her fate. I see Mr. Gladstone has wondered what should have induced Homer (whom he holds to have written the *Odyssey* as well as the *Iliad*) to tell us that Clytemnestra was a good woman to start with,[註2] but with all my respect for his great services to Homeric literature, I cannot think that he has hit upon the right explanation. It should not be forgotten, moreover, that this extenuation of Clytemnestra's guilt belongs to a part of the *Odyssey* that was engrafted on to the original design—a part in which, as I shall show later, there was another woman's guilt, which was only not extenuated because it was absolutely denied in the face of overwhelming evidence—I mean Penelope's.

〔U0707 para〕
第二個例子，是歐邁俄斯小時候把他拐走的那個女人。她做了這件事、跟著腓尼基商人上了船之後沒幾天，就被阿蒂蜜絲殺死，屍體被拋下船去餵海豹和魚（卷十五 403–484 行）。
> The second case in point is that of the woman who stole Eumæus when he was a child. A few days after she has done this, and has gone on board the ship with the Phœnician traders, she is killed by Diana, and thrown overboard to the seals and fishes (xv. 403-484).

〔U0708 para〕
第三個例子，是奧德修斯家中那些在他離家期間與求婚者胡來的女人。詩中告訴我們，家裡有五十個女僕，其中只有十二個有罪。奇怪的是，女僕的數目竟與阿爾基諾俄斯王家中的侍女數目完全相同；另外也該注意，考慮到求婚者有一百多人，而女僕們似乎只要願意，夜裡就能離開宅邸（卷二十 6–8 行），十二個有罪的女僕實在是個很小的數目──不錯，詩中別處告訴我們，那些女人是遭到姦污、被迫屈從的，但這樣一來，她們人數之少就更令人驚奇了──而且我還可以補一句：吊死她們也就更加殘酷可怕。我想，史前時代風氣的鬆弛，會促使一個對女性榮譽並不特別戒慎的作者說，有罪的有三十八個，甚至更多，清白的只有十二個。另一方面，我們也必須記住：奧德修斯殺死求婚者之後，歐律克蕾亞把那三十八個清白的女人帶出來見他，奧德修斯一一認出了她們（卷二十二 501 行）。所以她們當中最年輕的，也很難說會在四十歲以下，有些無疑還更老──因為奧德修斯已經離家二十年了。
> The third case is that of the women of Ulysses' household who had misconducted themselves with the suitors during his absence. We are told that there were fifty women servants in the house, of whom twelve alone were guilty. It is curious that the number of servants should be exactly the same as that of the maidservants in the house of king Alcinous, and it should be also noted that twelve is a very small number for the guilty servants, considering that there were over a hundred suitors, and that the maids seem to have been able to leave the house by night when they chose to do so (xx. 6-8)—true, we are elsewhere told that the women had been violated and only yielded under compulsion, but this makes it more wonderful that they should be so few—and I may add, more terribly severe to hang them. I think the laxity of prehistoric times would have prompted a writer who was not particularly jealous for the honour of woman, to have said that there were thirty-eight, or even more, guilty, and only twelve innocent. We must bear in mind on the other hand that when Euryclea brought out the thirty-eight innocent women to see Ulysses after he had killed the suitors, Ulysses recognised them all (xxii. 501). The youngest of them therefore can hardly have been under forty, and so me no doubt were older—for Ulysses had been gone twenty years.

〔U0709 para〕
那麼，有罪的女僕受到怎樣的處置？有個男人把我「《奧德賽》出自女人之手」的理論當作純粹的 *mauvaise plaisanterie*（拙劣玩笑），有一次對我說，這種說法荒唐透頂，因為求婚者被殺之後，女人第一個會想到的，是餐廳的地毯。我說，*mutatis mutandis*（作必要的變通之後），這正是她確實想到的事。
> Now how are the guilty ones treated? A man who was speaking of my theory that the *Odyssey* was written by a woman as a mere *mauvaise plaisanterie*, once told me it was absurd, for the first thing a woman would have thought of after the suitors had been killed was the dining room carpet. I said that *mutatis mutandis* this was the very thing she did think of.

〔U0710 para〕
奧德修斯一確定沒有一個求婚者還活著，就吩咐歐律克蕾亞把有罪的女僕帶來；她們到了以後，他對忒勒馬科斯、歐邁俄斯和菲羅提俄斯說（卷二十二 437–443 行）：──
> As soon as Ulysses has satisfied himself that not a single suitor is left alive, he tells Euryclea to send him the guilty maidservants, and on their arrival he says to Telemachus, Eumæus and Philœtius (xxii. 437-443):—

〔U0711 quote〕
「動手把屍體搬走，叫那些女人幫你們。做完之後，把座椅和桌子都用海綿擦洗乾淨，直到整座宅子收拾妥當；然後把女僕們帶到外面去……用你們的劍刺穿她們。」
> "Begin to bear away the corpses, and make the women help you. When you have done this, sponge down the seats and tables, till you have set the whole house in order; then take the maids outside....and thrust them through with your swords."

〔U0712 para〕
這些命令都被忠實執行：女僕們幫忙搬走屍體，又用海綿擦洗椅子和桌子，直到擦乾淨為止──奧德修斯就站在一旁盯著，不讓她們浪費片刻。這些做完之後，忒勒馬科斯（詩中告訴我們〔卷二十二 426–427 行〕，他的母親一直不許他對女僕發號施令）把她們帶到外面，吊死了她們（卷二十二 462 行），認為這比他父親規定的死法更不光彩──或許他也想到，比起用劍刺死她們，這樣要清理的血會少一些──不過請參看第 98 頁的註。作者用一行大半借自《伊里亞德》的詩告訴我們[註3]，她們的腳抽搐了一會兒，但時間不長；然而她對忒勒馬科斯究竟怎麼吊死她們的，概念極其模糊。至今還沒有哪位評註家能弄懂這一段；唯一的解釋似乎是：作者自己也不懂，而且也懶得去懂。只要知道那些女人顯然是被吊死了，也就夠了。
> These orders are faithfully obeyed; the maids help in the work of removing the bodies and they sponge the chairs and tables till they are clean—Ulysses standing over them and seeing that they lose no time. This done, Telemachus (whose mother, we are told (xxii. 426-427) had never yet permitted him to give orders to the female servants) takes them outside and hangs them (xxii. 462), as a more dishonourable death than the one his father had prescribed for them—perhaps also he may have thought he should have less blood to clean up than if he stabbed them—but see note on p. 98. The writer tells us in a line which she borrows in great part from the *Iliad*,[註3] that their feet move convulsively for a short time though not for very long, but her ideas of the way in which Telemachus hanged them are of the vaguest. No commentator has ever yet been able to understand it; the only explanation seems to be that the writer did not understand it herself, and did not care to do so. Let it suffice that the women were obviously hanged.

〔U0713 para〕
在基督教以前的時代，沒有哪個男性作者會認為那些女人的罪過需要如此可怕的懲罰。他或許會下令處死她們，卻不會把憤慨推到這種地步，讓她們先去清洗自己情夫的血。作者對求婚者固然兇狠，對那些女人卻兇狠得多。求婚者全被殺死之後，歐律克蕾亞開始對著他們發出勝利的歡呼，奧德修斯卻制止了她。他說：「住嘴，老婦人，對著死人的屍體誇口是不對的。」（卷二十二 411 行）同樣地，一直到那些女人即將被處死的那一刻，還要逼她們去做最駭人的差事，也是不對的；但作者似乎全然沒有意識到這一點：凡是女性的榮譽遭到同性之人玷污，再重的懲罰對她們都不算過分。
> No man writing in pre-Christian times would have considered the guilt of the women to require so horrible a punishment. He might have ordered them to be killed, but he would not have carried his indignation to the point of making them first clean up the blood of their paramours. Fierce as the writer is against the suitors, she is far more so against the women. When the suitors are all killed, Euryclea begins to raise a cry of triumph over them, but Ulysses checks her. "Hold your tongue, woman," he says, "it is ill bragging over the bodies of dead men" (xxii. 411). So also it is ill getting the most hideous service out of women up to the very moment when they are to be executed; but the writer seems to have no sense of this; where female honour has been violated by those of woman's own sex, no punishment is too bad for them.

〔U0714 para〕
《奧德賽》中讓我傾向於把它歸於女人之手的其他主要特徵，是一種為藝術而藝術式的、對小小謊言的喜愛，以及只要合她的意，就決心兩面都要占的態度。這一點似乎從來不曾困擾她。故事就擺在那裡，讀者愛信不信。她喜愛那些一戳就破的偽裝，喜愛那些自相拆台、誰也騙不了的故弄玄虛。不必往遠處找，單看卷一和卷三：雅典娜在這兩卷裡各編了一套煞有介事、細節俱全的故事──一次說她正載著一船鐵要到忒墨塞去，打算換銅回來（卷一 184 行）；另一次說她第二天早上要到考科涅斯人那裡，去討一筆拖欠她很久的大債（卷三 366 行）──然後，她費盡心思編出來的謊話還沒說完，她就化成一隻鷹飛上天空，自己露了底。順帶一提，如果她是在一座有屋頂的大廳裡，這兩次她都很難這麼做；但如果像我所設想的，她兩次都是在一道環繞露天庭院、有屋頂的迴廊裡，那就可以想像她能這麼做了。
> The other chief characteristics of the *Odyssey* which incline me to ascribe it to a woman are a kind of art for art's sake love of a small lie, and a determination to have things both ways whenever it suits her purpose. This never seems to trouble her. There the story is, and the reader may take it or leave it. She loves flimsy disguises and mystifications that stultify themselves, and mystify nobody. To go no further than books i. and iii., Minerva in each of these tells plausible stories full of circumstantial details, about her being on her way to Temesa with a cargo of iron and how she meant to bring back copper (i. 184), and again how she was going to the Cauconians on the following morning to recover a large debt that had been long owing to her (iii. 366), and then, before the lies she had been at such pains to concoct are well out of her mouth she reveals herself by flying into the air in the form of an eagle. This, by the way, she could not well do in either case if she was in a roofed hall, but might be conceived as doing if, as I suppose her to have been in both cases, she was in a roofed cloister that ran round an open court.

〔U0715 para〕
這幾次飛走，頗有平行五度的味道[註4]，如果還不算是不折不扣的平行八度的話；我不禁認為，作者若肯費心去找，本可以找到更圓滑的進行方式。不過這一點暫且不談：善意的小謊從第一卷到最後一卷不斷出現；瑣細的宗教儀節處處被一絲不苟地堅持，而這些儀節一旦辦妥，卻又相當不擇手段；對神明與徵兆的敬畏，以及對一般 *convenances*（禮法規矩）的敬重──這一切在我看來，都更像女人的筆法，而不像男人的。
> There is a flavour of consecutive fifths in these flights,[註4] if indeed they are not downright octaves, and I cannot but think that the writer would have found a smoother progression open to her if she had cared to look for one; but letting this pass, the way in which white lies occur from the first book to the last, the punctiliousness, omnipresent, with which small religious observances are insisted upon, coupled with not a little unscrupulousness when these have been attended to, the respect for gods and omens, and for the *convenances* generally—all these seem to me to be more characteristic of a woman's writing than a man's.

〔U0716 para〕
再者，忒勒馬科斯被看待得那麼認真；他那麼緊緊遵守自己的計畫；凡是他父親、他母親、雅典娜或任何負責任的人告訴他該做什麼，他總是一絲不苟地照做──只有一段例外，而那一段幾乎是 *verbatim*（逐字）照搬《伊里亞德》的[註5]；雅典娜怎樣把他打扮得俊美、怎樣對他說教；他怎樣不聲不響卻堪為楷模地善盡一切宗教、道德與社交上的本分──這一切在我看來，都指向一個結論：作者是個女人，而且是個年輕的女人。
> The seriousness, again, with which Telemachus is taken, the closeness with which he adheres to his programme, the precision with which he invariably does what his father, his mother, Minerva, or any responsible person tells him that he should do, except in one passage which is taken almost *verbatim* from the *Iliad*,[註5] the way in which Minerva beautifies him and preaches to him; the unobtrusive but exemplary manner in which he discharges all his religious, moral, and social duties—all seem to me to point in the direction of thinking that the writer is a woman and a young one.

〔U0717 para〕

> How does Minerva preach to him? When he has washed his hands in the sea he prays that she will help him on his intended voyage in search of news concerning his father. The goddess then comes up to him disguised as Mentor, and speaks as follows:

If, then, you take after him your voyage will not be fruitless, but unless you have the blood of Ulysses and Penelope in your veins I see no likelihood of your succeeding. Sons are seldom as good men as their fathers; they are generally worse not better; still, as you are not going to be either fool or coward henceforward, and are not entirely without some share of your father's wise discernment, I look with hope upon your undertaking" (ii. 270-280).

〔U0719 para〕
可憐的門托爾因此得了個老奶奶般的名聲，大概永遠甩不掉了。其實那不是門托爾，而是雅典娜。作者並沒有讓雅典娜說，女兒很少比得上母親。我小時候有一位非常親愛、慈祥的老姑媽，她正是這樣跟我說話的。「你放縱如水，」她會說，「必不得居首位。」我翻譯上面那段文字時，幾乎聽見她又在這麼說（還有更多同樣意思的話）。我的叔伯舅舅們跟我說話，卻完全不是這個樣子。
> Hence the grandmotherly reputation which poor Mentor is never likely to lose. It was not Mentor but Minerva. The writer does not make Minerva say that daughters were rarely as good women as their mothers were. I had a very dear kind old aunt who when I was a boy used to talk to me just in this way. "Unstable as water," she would say, "thou shalt not excel." I almost heard her saying it (and more to the same effect) when I was translating the passage above given. My uncles did not talk to me at all in the same way.

〔U0720 para〕
我不妨在這裡順帶一提──不過這個題目我會在後面一章更詳細地討論──雅典娜教訓忒勒馬科斯的整段時間裡，她一定知道他這一去不但無益，反而有害，因為照她自己的安排，奧德修斯就快要回來了；事實上，忒勒馬科斯還沒到家，他就已經回到伊薩卡了。
> I may add parenthetically here, but will deal with the subject more fully in a later chapter, that all the time Minerva was lecturing Telemachus she must have known that his going would be worse than useless, inasmuch as Ulysses was, by her own arrangements, on the very eve of his return; and indeed he was back again in Ithaca before Telemachus got home.

〔U0721 para〕
再看卷十八 215 行以下，潘妮洛碧因為他讓奧德修斯和伊羅斯打架而責備他的樣子。她說：──
> See, again, the manner in which Penelope scolds him in Book xviii. 215 &c., for having let Ulysses and Irus fight. She says:—

〔U0722 quote〕
「忒勒馬科斯，我怕你已不像從前那樣明理、那樣規矩了。你年紀小的時候，比現在更懂得分寸；可是現在你長大了，論身材和相貌，陌生人看了你，會以為你是富家子弟，你的行為卻遠不是它應有的樣子。剛才鬧成那樣，到底是怎麼回事？你怎麼能讓一個陌生人受到這麼可恥的虐待？他在我們家裡作為求援者，要是受了重傷，那會怎麼樣？這對你來說，必定是非常丟臉的事。」
> "Telemachus, I fear you are no longer so discreet and well conducted as you used to be. When you were younger you had a greater sense of propriety; now, however, that you are grown up, though a stranger to look at you would take you for the son of a well-to-do father as far as size and good looks go, your conduct is by no means what it should have been. What is all this disturbance that has been going on, and how came you to allow a stranger to be so disgracefully ill-treated? What would have happened if he had suffered serious injury while a suppliant in our house? Surely this would have been very discreditable to you."

〔U0723 para〕
我不相信有哪個男人能讓一位母親這樣女人味十足地責備她的兒子。
> I do not believe any man could make a mother rebuke her son so femininely.

〔U0724 para〕
再者，人們忠貞不渝地為一個失蹤了二十年的兒子哭個不停，儘管他們還剩下三個兒子[註6]；或者為一個從未見過面的兄弟哭泣[註7]──這正是那種對家庭生活之神聖的戒慎的一部分，而在這方面，女人往往比男人要求得更嚴。
> Again, the fidelity with which people go on crying incessantly for a son who has been lost to them for twenty years, though they have still three sons left,[註6] or for a brother whom they have never even seen,[註7] is part and parcel of that jealousy for the sanctity of domestic life, in respect of which women are apt to be more exacting than men.

〔U0725 para〕
然而，儘管如此，作者卻讓忒勒馬科斯毫不掩飾一個事實：他的怨恨主要不在於他母親據說受了虐待，也不在於他父親的死，而在於求婚者的揮霍在他自己口袋裡挖出的那個洞。他向同胞們求援時，談到降臨在他家的兩大災禍，說道：──
> And yet in spite of all this the writer makes Telemachus take no pains to hide the fact that his grievance is not so much the alleged ill-treatment of his mother, nor yet the death of his father, as the hole which the extravagance of the suitors is making in his own pocket. When demanding assistance from his fellow countrymen, he says, of the two great evils that have fallen upon his house:—

〔U0726 quote〕
「第一件，是我失去了我那位卓越的父親，他曾是在座各位的首領，對你們每一個人都像父親一樣。第二件嚴重得多，不久就會把我的家產徹底毀掉。你們當中所有首領的兒子都在糾纏我母親，要她違背自己的意願嫁給他們。他們不敢到她父親伊卡里俄斯那裡去，請他挑選他最中意的人，並為他的女兒備辦嫁妝；卻一天又一天賴在我父親的宅子裡，宰殺我們的牛、羊和肥山羊來大吃大喝，喝掉多少酒連想都不想一下。沒有哪份家產經得起這樣的揮霍（卷二 46–48 行）。」
> "The first of these is the loss of my excellent father, who was chief among all you here present and was like a father to every one of you. The second is much more serious, and ere long will be the utter ruin of my estate. The sons of all the chief men among you are pestering my mother to marry them against her will. They are afraid to go to her father Icarius, asking him to choose the one he likes best, and to provide marriage gifts for his daughter, but day after day they keep hanging about my father's house, sacrificing our oxen, sheep, and fat goats for their banquets, and never giving so much as a thought to the quantity of wine they drink. No estate can stand such recklessness (ii. 46-48)."

〔U0727 para〕
此外，在卷三和卷四裡，忒勒馬科斯設法打聽父親的消息，從頭到尾都很清楚：他真正想要的，是父親已死的證據，而不是父親還活著的證據；雖然這或許只是因為他對第二種可能已經絕望了。忒勒馬科斯對求婚者揮霍的憤慨，作者在整部詩中都明顯感同身受；她對此怒不可遏；或許是因為她在自己父親家中親眼見過這種浪費。在她對這件事所說的一切底下，我們似乎感覺到一股私人怨氣在隱隱作痛；我也常常想到：她在求婚者身上，同時看到了那些一夜又一夜來白吃白喝、占阿爾基諾俄斯那份不知節制的好脾氣的便宜的鄰居──他們最後大概會把他的家吃垮。
> Moreover it is clear throughout Books iii. and iv., in which Telemachus is trying to get news of his father, that what he really wants is evidence of his death, not of his being alive, though this may only be because he despairs of the second alternative. The indignation of Telemachus on the score of the extravagance of the suitors is noticeably shared by the writer all through the poem; she is furious about it; perhaps by reason of the waste she saw going on in her father's house. Under all she says on this head we seem to feel the rankling of a private grievance, and it often crosses my mind that in the suitors she also saw the neighbours who night after night came sponging on the reckless good nature of Alcinous, to the probable eventual ruin of his house.

〔U0728 para〕
女人、宗教和錢財，是《奧德賽》作者心中三個主導的念頭。在《伊里亞德》裡，*belli causa*（戰爭的起因）是一個女人，錢財只是細節，而最顯眼的是男人。在《奧德賽》裡，*belli causa* 主要是錢財，最顯眼的是女人──即使在她看似並不顯眼的時候，往往也是如此──這就像在《伊里亞德》中那些照理說是特洛伊人大勝亞該亞人的卷次裡，作者一直著墨最多的，卻是特洛伊人遭到的殺戮。
> Woman, religion, and money are the three dominant ideas in the mind of the writer of the *Odyssey*. In the *Iliad* the *belli causa* is a woman, money is a detail, and man is most in evidence. In the *Odyssey* the *belli causa* is mainly money, and woman is most in evidence—often when she does not appear to be so—just as in the books of the *Iliad* in which the Trojans are supposed to be most triumphant over the Achæans, it is the Trojans all the time whose slaughter is most dwelt upon.

〔U0729 para〕
奇怪的是，《奧德賽》的作者心中時時想著錢財，這部詩卻絲毫沒有顯示出它是從營利的角度寫成的；而《伊里亞德》裡錢財很少出現，卻處處顯示它是為了迎合某一批作者既厭惡又鄙視的聽眾而寫的──因此也就是著眼於錢財而寫的。
> It is strange that the *Odyssey*, in which money is so constantly present to the mind of the writer, should show not even the faintest signs of having been written from a business point of view, whereas the *Iliad*, in which money appears but little, abounds with evidence of its having been written to take with a certain audience whom the writer both disliked and despised—and hence of having been written with an eye to money.

〔U0730 para〕
現在我要進入這個問題：潘妮洛碧是不是正在被──容我這麼說──粉飾漂白？《奧德賽》給我們的、關於她行為的說法，是當時流行的說法嗎？還是作者在操弄一個截然不同的故事，給它換上另一副面孔──就像所有詩人在重述任何故事時都容易做的那樣？丁尼生──更不必提許多更早的作家──就對亞瑟王傳說做過這種事；那些傳說的原始形態，把我們帶進一種道德氛圍，與我們在《國王敘事詩》中遇到的那種氛圍，可以說是天差地別。
> I will now proceed to the question whether Penelope is being, if I may say so, whitewashed. Is the version of her conduct that is given us in the *Odyssey* the then current one, or is the writer manipulating a very different story, and putting another face on it—as all poets are apt to do with any story that they are re-telling? Tennyson, not to mention many earlier writers, has done this with the *Arthurian Legends*, the original form of which takes us into a moral atmosphere as different as can well be conceived from the one we meet with in the *Idylls of the King*.

〔U0731 para〕
假定《奧德賽》的作者可能選擇改寫一個她認為既侮辱她的性別、本身又令人作嘔的故事，這並沒有什麼不可能（因為其他例子讀者很容易就會想到，我無須引述）；問題在於：她究竟有沒有這麼做？在她覆蓋上去的那幅畫底下，是否透出一幅早先畫作的痕跡，而且清楚到足以讓人一眼看出原畫畫的是什麼？如果沒有，我就放棄我的論點；但如果有，我就會認為，荷馬時代的男人極不可能去承擔這項不可能的任務：把潘妮洛碧寫得既可信又貞潔。恐怕我認為，他更可能把她寫得比上一位處理這個題材的詩人筆下更黑，而不會費什麼力氣去把她漂白。
> There is no improbability (for other instances will occur to the reader so readily that I need not quote them) in the supposition that the writer of the *Odyssey* might choose to recast a story which she deemed insulting to her sex, as well as disgusting in itself; the question is, has she done so or not? Do traces of an earlier picture show up through the one she has painted over it, so distinctly as to make it obvious what the original picture represented? If they do not, I will give up my case, but if they do, I shall hold it highly improbable that a man in the Homeric age would undertake the impossible task of making Penelope at the same time plausible and virtuous. I am afraid I think he would be likely to make her out blacker than the last poet who had treated the subject, rather than be at any pains to whiten her.

〔U0732 para〕
最不可能被促使把潘妮洛碧寫得比傳聞中更好的，就是荷馬本人。她更好還是更壞，他根本不會在乎。他喜歡女人，但也喜歡逗弄她們；他絲毫沒有顯露出任何為女性榮譽而戒慎的跡象，也沒有想要普遍抬高女性的意思。他在這方面毫無跡象，正如他對奧德修斯家中女人所施懲罰的那種兇殘也毫無跡象──單憑這種兇殘本身，就足以讓人無法想像《伊里亞德》和《奧德賽》會出自同一人之手。
> Least of all would Homer himself have been prompted to make Penelope out better than report says she was. He would not have cared whether she was better or worse. He is fond of women, but he is also fond of teasing them, and he shows not the slightest signs of any jealousy for female honour, or of a desire to exalt women generally. He shows no more sign of this than he does of the ferocity with which punishment is inflicted on the women of Ulysses' household—a ferocity which is in itself sufficient to make it inconceivable that the *Iliad* and the *Odyssey* should be by the same person.
